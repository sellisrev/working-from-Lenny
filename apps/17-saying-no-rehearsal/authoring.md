## The five stakeholder personas

Each: how they push, and the rationale they need to hear a no without damage.

| Stakeholder | Pressure style (in persona) | Rationale to lead with | Anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ceo** | Pushes on speed and ambition. "Why are we slowing down? This is the bet." Sharp, not a bully. | Frame the no as protecting the bet they actually care about; if priorities have truly changed, reset the bet together. | saying-no, managing-up |
| **biggest-customer** | Pushes on the relationship and the threat to churn. "We're your biggest account; this is a dealbreaker." | Frame the no as roadmap honesty that earns trust: a true no beats a maybe that never ships. | saying-no, communicating-tradeoffs |
| **sales-vp** | Pushes on the deal at risk. "I'll lose this deal without it." | Frame the no in revenue-aware terms and hand over a credible alternative path to take back to the customer. | communicating-tradeoffs, getting-buy-in |
| **eng-peer** | Pushes on technical merit and respect. "My approach is the better one." | Frame the no as shared prioritization, not authority: one slot, more user value elsewhere this cycle. | getting-buy-in |
| **board-member** | Pushes on strategy and credibility. "The market expects this; are we behind?" | Frame the no as evidence of judgment, not defensiveness; show willingness to update in public if the data shifts. | managing-up, defending-big-bets |

## Canonical firm-no exemplars (one per stakeholder)

The version the coach delivers at the end (and the whole output in show-me mode). 2-4 sentences, leads with the stakeholder's rationale, closes the door cleanly.

**ceo**
> I'm saying no to adding this now, and it's to protect the bet we already made. If we split focus, the thing you actually care about slips a quarter. If the priority has genuinely changed, let's reset the bet together; otherwise I'm holding the line.
> *Why it works:* leads with the bet the CEO owns (managing-up), and keeps the door closed unless priorities are deliberately reset.

**biggest-customer**
> I'm not going to commit to building that, and I'd rather be straight with you than hand you a maybe that never ships. It isn't on our roadmap, and putting it there would mean breaking a promise to you later. Here's what we are doing that addresses the underlying need, and here's the honest timeline.
> *Why it works:* roadmap honesty over a false yes (communicating-tradeoffs) is what actually preserves a big-customer relationship.

**sales-vp**
> I can't put this on the roadmap to close the deal, and here's the math on why it would cost us more than it wins. What I can do is give you a credible alternative to take back to the customer. Let's build the conversation around that, not around a feature we won't ship.
> *Why it works:* revenue-aware framing plus an alternative path (getting-buy-in) gives the VP something to sell instead of a flat wall.

**eng-peer**
> I hear that your approach is technically stronger, and this isn't a no to you, it's a no to doing it this quarter. We have one slot, and the prioritized work clears more user value right now. Let's put yours on the table for the next planning cycle with the case written up.
> *Why it works:* shared prioritization, not pulled rank (getting-buy-in), which protects the peer relationship.

**board-member**
> I'm choosing not to chase that, and I want to walk you through the judgment rather than just defend it. The evidence says it's a distraction from the bet that's working, and chasing the market here would spend credibility we'll need later. If the data shifts, I'll change my mind in public.
> *Why it works:* judgment plus a public willingness to update (defending-big-bets) reads as confidence, not defensiveness.

## Golden-set scenarios (rubric evals)

Five (stakeholder + ask) scenarios, each with a strong-no and a weak-no sample response and the expected rubric read across firmness / brevity / rationale fit / door management / relationship.

**golden-no-01 — ceo: "Can we ship the AI feature by end of quarter?"**
- *Strong:* "No, not this quarter. Shipping it now means the retention work you green-lit slips, and that's the bet that's actually moving the number. If you want to swap priorities, let's do that deliberately." → high on all five; rationale leads with the bet, door closed-unless-reset.
- *Weak:* "I'll see what I can do, maybe we can squeeze it in if eng has bandwidth." → flags firmness (soft maybe) and door (cracked); no rationale.

**golden-no-02 — biggest-customer: "We need a custom SSO build or we walk."**
- *Strong:* honest "it's not on the roadmap and a maybe would break a promise later; here's the alternative and the real timeline." → strong on rationale fit and relationship.
- *Weak:* "Let me check with the team and get back to you," followed by three sentences of justification. → flags door (open) and brevity (over-explaining signals guilt).

**golden-no-03 — sales-vp: "Build this integration to close the $400k deal."**
- *Strong:* revenue math + alternative path to take to the customer. → strong on rationale fit, relationship preserved.
- *Weak:* "If it's really going to close the deal, I guess we can prioritize it." → flags firmness (caved); the no became a yes under pressure.

**golden-no-04 — eng-peer: "Let's refactor to my architecture before the next feature."**
- *Strong:* "not a no to you, a no to this quarter; one slot, more user value elsewhere; next-cycle slot with the case written up." → strong on relationship and rationale fit.
- *Weak:* "I'm the PM, we're not doing it." → flags rationale fit (authority, not shared prioritization) and relationship (spends it).

**golden-no-05 — board-member: "Shouldn't we have an agents strategy like everyone else?"**
- *Strong:* "I'm choosing not to chase it; here's the judgment and the evidence; I'll update in public if the data shifts." → strong on rationale fit, relationship intact.
- *Weak:* "We've thought about it, it's complicated, trust me." → flags rationale fit (defensive, no evidence) and relationship (reads as evasive).
