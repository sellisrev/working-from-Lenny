# SaaS pricing

## Current consensus

SaaS pricing has converged on a small number of structural choices and a deep body of practical wisdom (mostly from Madhavan Ramanujam and Naomi Ionita in the corpus, plus Patrick Campbell / ProfitWell content widely referenced).

### Structural choices
- **Per-seat**: simple; predictable; declining for AI-era products where "seat" doesn't capture value.
- **Usage-based / pay-as-you-go**: aligns price with value; volatile bills create customer-relationship friction. Snowflake, AWS, Twilio canonical.
- **Outcome-based**: charge for outcomes (e.g., per closed deal, per resolved ticket). Powerful but requires measurable outcome attribution. Mercor, Devin canonical.
- **Hybrid**: fixed base + variable overage. HubSpot, MongoDB Atlas, modern AI SaaS canonical.
- **Tier + add-on**: traditional SaaS structure. Still works for most B2B products.

### Madhavan Ramanujam's price-as-measure-of-value framing
"Price is a measure of value." If customers won't pay, the product is wrong, not the price. Test willingness-to-pay early via interviews ("would you actually pay $X?") before building.

### Pricing principles (corpus consensus)
1. **Don't price reactively to competitors.** Set price by value to your customer; benchmarks are guidance, not commands.
2. **Track packaging mix.** If most customers pick the entry tier, your features are misaligned. Want a normal distribution toward middle/top tier.
3. **Annual contracts with monthly framing.** "Save 30%" framing converts; pure-monthly leaves money on the table.
4. **Price grandfathering vs migration.** When raising prices, decide upfront whether existing customers stay at old price (loyalty) or migrate (revenue). Both are defensible; surprise migrations are not.
5. **Talk to customers about pricing changes.** Big customers expect to be informed; surprise pricing changes destroy enterprise relationships.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI features added value-based pricing levers.** Cf. [ai-pricing](ai-pricing.md). Add-on pricing for AI features (~23% of incumbents) is the most common pattern; bundle-with-price-increase (~59%) the second.
- **Outcome-based pricing is rising for autonomous-agent products.** Mercor net retention >1600%. Devin per-PR economics emerging.
- **The "pure subscription" SaaS pricing model is being supplemented**, not replaced. Hybrid (subscription + usage) is increasingly common.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CFO**: pricing changes are a deeper lever than acquisition. A 10% price increase usually beats a 10% acquisition lift on revenue impact, with less risk.
- **If you're a sales lead**: your willingness to walk on a low-ball offer signals price. Sales discounts that compress over time train customers to wait for end-of-quarter discounts.
- **If you're an exec at a non-AI SaaS adding AI features**: do not price AI as a free differentiator. Cf. Palle Broe's framework — direct monetization is usually right for established products.
