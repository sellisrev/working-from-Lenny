# Conversion rate optimization

## Current consensus

Conversion is a downstream symptom; durable wins come from upstream causes. The corpus consensus:

1. **Define conversion narrowly.** "Sign-up to first activation" is more useful than "visitor to user." The narrower the funnel step, the more actionable the optimization.
2. **Optimize cohorts, not aggregates.** A 5% lift on aggregate conversion can hide a 20% drop on a specific segment that matters most. Cf. [cohort-retention](cohort-retention.md).
3. **Behavioral science levers work** but with diminishing returns. Cialdini-style influence (social proof, scarcity, reciprocity) generally lifts conversion 2-15%. Don't over-rely on these tactics; they wear out.
4. **The bigger lever is fit, not friction.** Strategy and tactics for increasing conversion (Lenny, 2020): the highest-impact moves are usually about who you target and what you show them, not about button copy.
5. **Waitlist conversion has its own rules.** "Good" waitlist conversion is 5-30% depending on category; 50%+ is unusual. Lenny's waitlist piece (2022) is the canonical reference.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-driven personalization** is now table stakes for high-stakes conversion paths (checkout, signup, pricing pages). Per-user variant testing has gotten cheaper.
- **Conversion funnels for AI products** behave differently — users churn at the moment they hit model capability limits rather than at traditional friction points. Cf. [retention](retention.md).

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a marketing or growth lead**: don't chase conversion lifts in isolation. Pair them with retention (cf. [retention](retention.md)) — a +20% conversion lift that hurts retention is net negative.
- **If you're a CFO**: conversion lift on an unprofitable funnel is unprofitable growth. Track CAC payback alongside conversion rate.
- **If you're an engineer or designer**: conversion experiments often fail because the test was poorly designed (small sample, novelty effects, dependency confounds). Don't ship based on a single test result; require replication.
