# Getting promoted as a PM

## Current consensus

Promotions follow a small number of patterns. Corpus consensus:

### The mechanics
1. **Operate at the next level for 6+ months before the promotion.** "Promote when ready" companies promote based on observed behavior, not perceived potential.
2. **Be visible to the people who decide.** Your manager + their peers. Skip-level relationships matter.
3. **Document impact, not output.** Numbers, business outcomes, team-level effects — not features shipped.
4. **Pick projects that demonstrate next-level skills.** A senior PM in 2026 can ship features cross-functionally; that's not promotable to staff. A staff PM influences strategy across multiple teams; that's promotable to principal/director.
5. **Don't wait to be sponsored.** Make the case yourself; provide the evidence.

### The political reality
- **Promotion decisions involve more politics than performance reviews suggest.** Your manager championing you matters; your manager being respected by leadership matters.
- **The "I deserve it" framing rarely wins.** "Here's the evidence I'm operating at the level" + "here's the impact" wins.
- **If your manager isn't championing you**, you have a manager problem more than a performance problem. The fix is rarely getting better at your current job.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **Builder PMs are promoted faster** at AI-native companies. The "shipped X impactful thing" promotion criterion has overtaken "led X complex cross-functional project" as the dominant signal.
- **The information-mover PM career ceiling is dropping.** Cf. [ai-replacing-pms](ai-replacing-pms.md). PMs whose primary value-add is coordination are increasingly capped at senior; staff/principal increasingly require shipped product impact.
- **Founder-track is more legitimate.** Cf. [becoming-senior-pm](becoming-senior-pm.md). Some senior PMs now skip the promotion ladder entirely and found companies.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a manager promoting your reports**: be explicit about what next-level looks like. Fuzzy criteria are hostile to high-performing direct reports.
- **If you're an engineer or designer wondering when to ask for a promotion**: same pattern as PM. Operate at next level, document impact, make the case.
- **If you're a CFO reviewing promotion budget**: 1-2 strong promotions per cycle is healthier than rubber-stamping the cohort. Promotion inflation devalues senior titles.
