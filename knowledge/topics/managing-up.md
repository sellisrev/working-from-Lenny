# Managing up

## Current consensus

Managing up is the practice of making your manager and skip-level effective stewards of your work. The corpus consensus, anchored in Lenny's "Managing up" canon (2021) plus Wes Kao communication content (2024):

### The core principles
1. **Make your manager's job easier.** Bring solutions; flag risks before they're crises; be the report whose 1:1 leaves them better, not more anxious.
2. **Match their communication style.** Some managers want bullets; some want narrative; some want async docs. Adapt to them.
3. **Surface the right altitude of detail.** Too much detail wastes their time; too little leaves them unable to help. Calibrate.
4. **Update them on what they need to know, not what you've done.** Status is not value-add; insight is.
5. **Disagree with reasoning, not emotion.** Wes Kao's framing on communication: "I think we should do X because Y" beats "I don't agree."
6. **Ask for what you need.** Promotions, resources, air cover — managers can't read minds. Make explicit asks.

### The Wes Kao additions (2024)
- **Lead with the headline.** First sentence of any update is the takeaway.
- **Specificity wins.** Numbers, dates, named decisions.
- **Pre-write hard conversations.** Draft what you'd say; revise; then have the meeting.

### Common failure modes
- **Over-updating with status.** Trip reports, weekly notes that are activity logs not insight.
- **Hiding bad news.** Always backfires. Cf. [communicating-bad-news](communicating-bad-news.md).
- **Going around your manager to skip-level.** Once a pattern, the manager relationship is broken.
- **Not actually managing them.** Passive recipients of direction don't develop influence.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling reduces "what did you do this week?" overhead.** Auto-generated activity summaries free up 1:1s for higher-leverage conversations.
- **Manager-of-manager interactions matter more.** Singhal's bifurcation framing: builders are seen / promoted by skip-level + above; managing skip-level relationships is increasingly load-bearing for senior+ careers.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineer or designer**: same playbook applies. Engineers especially under-invest in this and pay long-term career costs.
- **If you're a manager being managed-up well**: notice and reward it. Most reports won't, and the ones who do are leverage multipliers.
- **If you're an exec wondering why some teams seem to function better**: often the difference is reports who manage up well, not managers who manage well.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2021-2022**: Lenny "Managing up" canon.
- **2024**: Wes Kao on communication-as-influence; specific tactical patterns.
- **2025-2026**: AI tooling reframes the routine status-update part of managing up.
