# A three-step framework for solving problems

## Current consensus

Lenny's "A Three-Step Framework For Solving Problems" (2017, foundational) is the corpus-canonical generic problem-solving approach.

### The three steps
1. **Frame the problem.** Diagnose what's actually broken; resist the urge to skip to solutions. Cf. Rumelt's "diagnosis" in [Good Strategy / Bad Strategy](../books/good-strategy-bad-strategy.md).
2. **Generate options.** At least 3-5 distinct approaches. Don't fall in love with the first one.
3. **Decide and commit.** Pick one (with rationale); commit to executing fully before evaluating.

### Why this works
- **Skipping framing is the most common mistake.** Most "problems" are mis-framed; the real problem is upstream.
- **Single-option thinking is solution fixation.** Generating multiple options reveals tradeoffs the single-option path hides.
- **Commit-and-execute beats deliberate-and-flip-flop.** Imperfect commitment usually beats perfect indecision.

### How this connects to other corpus frameworks
- **Roger Martin's 5 questions** (cf. [evaluating-product-bets](evaluating-product-bets.md), [assessing-product-strategy-as-board](assessing-product-strategy-as-board.md)) is the strategy-specific elaboration.
- **Annie Duke's pre-mortem** (cf. [decision-making-frameworks](decision-making-frameworks.md)) is what you do before "decide and commit."
- **Eigenquestions** (Shishir Mehrotra, 2024 episode) — the framing step often hinges on identifying the right question.

### Common failure modes
- **Solution-mode default.** Skipping framing because the team is "action-oriented."
- **Analysis paralysis.** Spending too long in framing/options and never deciding.
- **Reversibility blindness.** Treating Type-2 (reversible) decisions as if they were Type-1 (irreversible).

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling enables faster option generation.** What took a half-day workshop is now a 15-minute prompt session. The bottleneck is judgment, not generation.
- **The framework is unchanged.** The generic problem-solving structure is durable.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineer**: pause on bug-fixes and feature requests. "What's the actual problem?" framing 5 minutes upstream often saves hours downstream.
- **If you're an exec running team meetings**: enforce the three steps. "Are we framing or solving?" is a useful interrupt.
- **If you're a CEO**: most strategic crises are framing failures. Cf. [first-principles](first-principles.md).

## How thinking has evolved

- **2017**: Lenny "A Three-Step Framework For Solving Problems" — corpus foundational piece.
- **2023-2024**: Shishir Mehrotra (Coda) on eigenquestions; Annie Duke on decision quality.
- **2025-2026**: AI-tooling speeds option generation; framework unchanged.
