# Communicating tradeoffs to leaders

## Current consensus

The corpus framing, anchored in Lenny's "How to communicate tradeoffs so leaders will listen" (2024):

### The framework
1. **Name the goal explicitly.** What outcome are we optimizing for? If you can't, the tradeoff conversation will spiral.
2. **Surface 2-3 options, not 1.** A single recommendation feels like an ask; 2-3 options frames it as a choice.
3. **Quantify each option.** Cost, benefit, risk, time. Numbers ground the conversation.
4. **Make the asymmetry visible.** "Option A is faster but riskier; Option B is slower but safer." Don't let the leader infer the asymmetry.
5. **Recommend.** After laying out options, name your preferred path. "I recommend B because Y."
6. **Be explicit about confidence.** "High confidence on A's risk; medium confidence on B's timeline."

### Common failure modes
- **Presenting tradeoffs as problems.** "We have a choice to make" — don't deliver the framing as a complaint.
- **Hiding your recommendation.** Leaders want your judgment; don't waste their time with "you decide."
- **Optimizing for never being wrong.** A wishy-washy tradeoff communication loses leader trust.
- **Skipping the data.** Pure-narrative tradeoffs are weaker than numbers + narrative.

### The Wes Kao layer
- **Lead with the headline recommendation**, then walk back to the options.
- **Use "because" framing.** Tradeoffs land when the audience can connect them to their priorities.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-augmented tradeoff analysis.** Models can produce option-comparison matrices quickly; the bottleneck is judgment about what to consider, not analysis effort.
- **Live prototypes for tradeoff comparison.** Cf. Aparna Chennapragada / Cat Wu — sometimes a working A vs. working B prototype beats any verbal tradeoff explanation.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineer presenting technical tradeoffs**: same playbook. Engineers underspecify the business-impact dimension; PMs underspecify the technical dimension. Both lose.
- **If you're an exec being presented tradeoffs**: insist on options + recommendation + confidence levels. Punish single-option presentations gently; reward 2-3-option ones.
- **If you're a CFO or board member**: tradeoff communications without quantification are weak; ask for numbers.
