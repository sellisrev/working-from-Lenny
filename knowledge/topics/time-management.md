# Time management techniques

## Current consensus

Lenny's "Time management techniques that actually work" two-part series (2024) is the corpus-canonical synthesis, plus the Knapp & Zeratsky *Make Time* canon and Lenny "Why no productivity hack will solve your overwhelm" counter-take.

### The corpus-consensus stack
1. **Prioritize ruthlessly.** Not every task deserves time; most don't. Cut, then schedule.
2. **Time-block.** Calendar is reality; intent without scheduled time doesn't survive.
3. **Single-task.** Multitasking destroys output. Block long single-focus periods (1-2 hours minimum).
4. **Energy management > time management.** Match high-cognitive-demand work to high-energy times.
5. **Saying no protects time.** Cf. [saying-no](saying-no.md). Yes-by-default is the most expensive habit in tech.

### Make Time (Knapp & Zeratsky) layer
- **Highlight**: pick one priority for the day; everything else is bonus.
- **Laser**: kill distractions during the highlight work.
- **Energize**: care for body — sleep, exercise, food.
- **Reflect**: end of day, did the highlight happen? Why or why not?

### The counter-take (Lenny "Why no productivity hack will solve your overwhelm")
- **Overwhelm is usually not a time problem.** Often it's a saying-no problem, an alignment problem, or an emotional problem dressed up as time pressure.
- **No tool fixes a culture of constant context-switching.** If your org demands availability across meetings, Slack, email, hallway, the productivity tool wars are theater.
- **The fix is structural.** Boundaries, expectations, async culture.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling reduces some tasks** (drafting, meeting summarization, status updates). Time gains are real but often re-spent on more ambitious work.
- **Always-on culture has gotten worse, not better, in some companies.** AI doesn't fix bad org culture.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a manager**: don't preach productivity; fix the structural reasons people are overwhelmed.
- **If you're a CEO**: model time boundaries. Don't send Slack messages at 11pm if you don't want a culture of 11pm Slack.
- **If you're an individual struggling**: examine the saying-no question first; productivity tools second.
