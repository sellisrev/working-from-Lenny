# PM influence without authority

## Current consensus

PMs operate without formal authority over engineers, designers, or sales — yet they're accountable for product outcomes. The corpus has converged on five high-leverage influence skills:

1. **Build trust through reliability.** Fareed Mosavat ([How to build trust and grow as a product leader](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-build-trust-and-grow)): trust is the substrate; without it, every influence attempt fails. Reliability comes from consistent follow-through on small commitments.

2. **Lead with the why, not the what.** Engineers and designers cooperate when they understand the goal. PMs who issue specs without context get compliance, not engagement.

3. **Make the trade-offs explicit.** Cf. [communicating-tradeoffs](communicating-tradeoffs.md). Don't ask people to do work without showing what's being given up. Trade-off transparency builds influence.

4. **Use prototypes to settle arguments.** Aparna Chennapragada and Cat Wu both: bring a prototype to a debate, not a deck. The prototype reveals truth that text discussion can't.

5. **Have conviction, but update it on evidence.** Mihika Kapoor (Figma, "Vision, conviction, and hype"): hold strong opinions weakly. PMs who flip-flop on every piece of feedback have no influence; PMs who never update are dismissed.

The Wes Kao / Jeffrey Pfeffer / Carolyn Robin tradition adds tactics:
- **Anchor on outcomes, not output.**
- **Speak the language of your audience** (engineers want context; designers want user need; sales wants pipeline impact; execs want strategic linkage).
- **Choose battles deliberately**; not every disagreement merits a fight.

## What's changed in 2025-2026

- **Conviction matters more than ever.** Nikhyl Singhal: builders are wanted, information movers shed. The "builder" archetype carries personal conviction about what to ship; influence flows from that conviction rather than from process orchestration.
- **The "PM as CEO of the product"** framing (criticized when overplayed — cf. Keith Rabois cautions) gains nuance: CEO-style ownership comes with responsibility to make calls, not authority to override others. Influence is the right vehicle.
- **For AI features**: influence requires technical engagement. PMs who can't explain why a model can't do X yet lose credibility with engineers; PMs who over-promise model capability lose credibility on the next miss.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineer working with a PM whose influence approach you find off-putting**: the four most common patterns are framework-obsession, dictation, decision-avoidance, and credit-hoarding (cf. [spotting-bad-pm-behaviors](spotting-bad-pm-behaviors.md)). Direct feedback is the first move; escalation if direct feedback fails.
- **If you're a sales lead frustrated by a PM's "no"**: the way to gain influence with a PM is to bring data and patterns, not anecdotes. Cf. [working-with-pms-as-sales](working-with-pms-as-sales.md).
- **If you're an exec evaluating a PM**: their influence shows in cross-functional work shipping smoothly without escalation. PMs who escalate every disagreement to leadership lack influence.
