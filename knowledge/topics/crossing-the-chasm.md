# Crossing the chasm and beachhead segment selection

## Current consensus

Geoffrey Moore's *Crossing the Chasm* framework remains the canonical model for B2B startups bridging from early adopters (innovators + early adopters) to the early majority. The corpus consensus:

### The chasm
- **Innovators + early adopters** buy on vision; they're forgiving of incomplete product.
- **Early majority** buys on references and proof from people like them. They don't share innovators' tolerance for incomplete product.
- **The chasm** is the gap. Many startups die there because what worked for innovators doesn't translate to the early majority.

### Beachhead strategy
Pick a single niche segment — narrow enough that you can credibly dominate it. Win it completely. Use that domination as the springboard to adjacent segments. The mistake is targeting "everyone in the early majority" — you fail to dominate any segment.

### Geoffrey Moore's framework for crossing
1. **Pick the beachhead segment.** Specific industry vertical + use case + buyer.
2. **Build the whole product** for that segment. Not just code; documentation, integrations, references, support — everything they need.
3. **Win it completely** — 30%+ market share in the segment is the threshold.
4. **Use the success to bridge to adjacent segments.** "We won healthcare; now we expand to financial services."

### Common failure modes
- **Picking too broad a beachhead.** "B2B SaaS" is not a segment; "mid-market HR-tech for hospitality" is.
- **Trying to cross the chasm with the innovator pitch.** Doesn't work. The early majority needs different evidence and framing.
- **Skipping the beachhead entirely.** Going broad pre-PMF dilutes resources.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-native B2B startups have compressed the chasm crossing.** Cursor went from innovator adoption to mainstream developer use in 18 months. The chasm still exists but is narrower for products with clearly self-evident value.
- **AI changes the "whole product" definition.** What counts as a complete product for the early majority increasingly includes AI features. B2B products without AI integrations face an "incomplete product" perception even if their core works.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO at a Series A B2B**: pick the beachhead explicitly and document it. "We're targeting hospitality SaaS HR managers at companies of 50-500 employees" is the level of specificity. Vaguer is failure.
- **If you're an investor evaluating a Series A**: ask the team to name their beachhead and their next 2 adjacent segments. Inability to answer is a red flag.
- **If you're a sales / GTM lead**: aligning your sales motion with the beachhead is the highest-leverage move. Calling outside the beachhead wastes pipeline.
- **If you're a non-PM hearing "we want to cross the chasm"**: ask how the whole-product investment plan supports that crossing. References, training materials, partner ecosystem, customer support — these are the chasm investments.

## How thinking has evolved

- **Pre-corpus**: Geoffrey Moore's *Crossing the Chasm* (1991, multiple revisions). Foundational.
- **2024**: Geoffrey Moore on Lenny's Podcast — finding your beachhead, crossing the chasm, dominating a market.
- **2024-2026**: AI-native compression of chasm timelines; principles unchanged.
