# Working with PMs as a designer

## Current consensus

Katie Dill's five-tip framework (Stripe Head of Design, ex-Lyft, ex-Airbnb; 2021 newsletter; reinforced in her 2024 podcast appearance) remains the canonical playbook for how PMs should work with designers. Inverted, it's also the playbook for designers evaluating their PMs.

Five things you want from your PM (Katie Dill, 2021):

1. **Trust the designer's expertise.** Don't pre-determine the design outcome before involving design. Hand over user-need framing, not pixel direction.
2. **Be the conductor.** PMs orchestrate cross-functional flow; let design own the design.
3. **Include designers early.** From "what is the problem?" not from "design this UI we already specified."
4. **Embrace research.** User research belongs in the team's daily reasoning, not as an annual deck.
5. **Communicate the why.** Designers do better work when they understand business context, not just feature specs.

The 2026 update:

- **PMs are now expected to ship prototypes too** (Aparna Chennapragada, Cat Wu). For designers this is a bigger shift than for engineers. Designers should welcome PM prototypes as input to the design conversation, not feel territorial about them. Ravi Mehta's "start with JSON" approach also reframes design's role: design works on the *experience* the JSON enables, not on producing the JSON itself.
- **Vibe-coded prototypes are not finished design.** v0 / Bolt / Lovable produce serviceable mocks; they do not produce design craft. Hold the line on the difference. Prototype-as-input is welcomed; prototype-as-deliverable is a regression.
- **The role boundary is dissolving.** Cat Wu: "designers PM and ship code." Many of the AI-native company designers are also doing PM work. Don't fight the boundary; renegotiate the role around the work that needs doing.

## What this means for designers specifically

- **A PM who hands you specs with screen mockups attached is failing at #1 above.** Push back; ask for the user need, not the UI proposal.
- **A PM who excludes you from the "what should we build?" phase and only invites you for "design what we decided" is a Coordinator pattern** ([spotting-bad-pm-behaviors](spotting-bad-pm-behaviors.md)). Push back early; if it persists, escalate.
- **When the PM ships their own prototype, evaluate it on intent, not craft.** Their goal is to communicate, not to compete with you. The prototype tells you what they think the product should do; respond by designing the experience that delivers it well.
- **Hold the line on user research.** PMs who skip research are flying blind. Make the research process visible; share what you're learning; bring designers' research into roadmap decisions, not just into design reviews.
- **For AI features**: design has a new responsibility for NLX (natural language interface) patterns (Aparna Chennapragada framing). What do plans, status, follow-ups, errors look like? These are design problems, not just engineering problems.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2021-03-23**: Katie Dill — five-tip framework. Foundational. ([The PM Design Partnership](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-pm--design-partnership))
- **2024-04**: Katie Dill returns on podcast — "Building beautiful products with Stripe's Head of Design"; reinforces 2021 framing with Stripe's working-process specifics.
- **2025-05-18**: Aparna Chennapragada — NLX framing; design has new responsibility for natural-language UX.
- **2025-09-29**: Ravi Mehta — JSON-first prototyping. Design works on the experience layered on the data model.
- **2026-04-23**: Cat Wu — at Anthropic, designers PM and ship code; the role boundary is purposefully dissolved.

## Inflection points

- **Katie Dill's 2021 piece**: codified the PM-design partnership for the corpus.
- **AI prototyping ubiquity (2025-2026)**: PMs ship prototypes; design adapts.
- **Cat Wu's "skills are merging" framing (April 2026)**: explicit policy framing of the boundary dissolution.
