# Giving feedback to PMs / cross-functional partners

For people managers (eng managers, design leads, sales managers, exec leaders) giving feedback to product managers and other cross-functional partners.

## Current consensus

Two frameworks dominate the corpus:

### 1. Radical Candor (Kim Scott, 2017 book → 2024 podcast appearance)

Two axes: **care personally** and **challenge directly**. The 2x2:
- High care + high challenge = Radical Candor (the goal)
- Low care + high challenge = Obnoxious Aggression (you've all seen it)
- High care + low challenge = Ruinous Empathy (the most common failure mode for managers)
- Low care + low challenge = Manipulative Insincerity

The most common pitfall: ruinous empathy. People-pleaser managers who can't deliver hard feedback do their reports a disservice.

### 2. The GAIN framework (Lenny + Therese Huston, 2025)

A more recent corpus addition. Stands for: **Goal**, **Ask**, **Impact**, **Next steps**. The full piece: "[Introducing the GAIN framework for feedback](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/introducing-the-gain-framework-for-feedback-an-evidence-based-approach-to-giving)." Evidence-based; engineered to maximize the chance the feedback is heard and acted on.

### The 2026 layer: Alisa Cohn's scripts (Dec 2024)

Alisa Cohn ("Scripts for difficult conversations") provides specific verbal scripts for giving hard feedback, navigating defensiveness, and ending meetings with three useful questions. These are the most practical-tactical resource in the corpus for the actual delivery moment.

## Practical playbook for giving feedback to a PM

- **Lead with the impact, not the behavior.** "When you skipped the cross-functional review, the eng team had to redo X" lands better than "you didn't follow process."
- **Time-box hard feedback.** Schedule a dedicated conversation; don't ambush. The person needs cognitive bandwidth to receive it.
- **Distinguish coachable from non-coachable.** Some patterns are coachable in 90 days (Coordinator pattern, framework-obsession). Some aren't (deep Dictator pattern, persistent integrity issues). Calibrate.
- **For PMs with a long-tenure performance gap**: probably not a quick coaching fix. Make a managed-transition decision rather than dragging out feedback indefinitely.
- **For new-PM struggles**: Lenny's [most common pitfalls](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-most-common-pitfalls-of-new-product-managers) is a roadmap. Most are coachable.
- **Document the pattern.** A single example doesn't justify a hard feedback conversation; a pattern does.
- **Ask what they think first.** Often the PM already knows; making them say it first shifts the conversation from "I'm telling you you failed" to "we're figuring this out together."
- **Follow up on agreed actions.** Feedback without follow-through is theater.

## What this means for non-PM cross-functional managers giving feedback

- **If you're an eng manager managing engineers who work with a PM**: feedback to the PM should ideally come from their PM manager, not you directly. Channel observed patterns through the PM management chain. If escalation is needed, frame it as cross-functional collaboration concerns, not performance review.
- **If you're a design lead** with a struggling cross-functional PM partner: same channeling logic. Document patterns; share with both your manager and the PM's manager.
- **If you're a sales / GTM lead** giving feedback on a PM who's blocking deals: be specific (deal sizes, what was missing) and avoid framing it as "you failed sales" — frame it as a roadmap-customer-mismatch question.
- **If you're an exec / founder** giving feedback to a head of product: be willing to be specific. "I want better PMs" is not feedback. "Here are three PRs / decisions / behaviors I disagree with and why" is.
