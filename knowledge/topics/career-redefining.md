# Redefining success and the pathless path

## Current consensus

The corpus has a notable thread on questioning the conventional career ladder. Anchored in Paul Millerd (*The Pathless Path*, 2024), Graham Weaver ("How to break out of autopilot", 2024-2025), and Jerry Colonna ("How have I been complicit in creating the conditions I say I don't want?").

### The core questions
1. **What are you actually optimizing for?** Most people pursue the next promotion / next role without examining whether the destination is what they want.
2. **What would you do if status didn't matter?** Stripping social-proof signals out reveals what you actually value.
3. **What are you complicit in?** Jerry Colonna's framing: the conditions you complain about are partly your own creation. What would changing your behavior change?
4. **What's your "second mountain"?** The first mountain (career success, money, status) is one journey; the second mountain (meaning, contribution, relationships) is different. Most people switch mountains too late.

### The pathless path (Paul Millerd)
- Leaving the conventional ladder isn't necessarily quitting; it's redefining what success looks like.
- Most pathless-path stories involve multi-year transitions, not abrupt jumps.
- Financial independence varies; you don't need to be FIRE'd to redefine, but baseline financial cushion helps.

### What makes the corpus version of this thread distinctive
- **Not anti-ambition.** Multiple guests redefined success after substantial career achievement, not before.
- **Skeptical of pure burnout / quit-everything narratives.** Corpus emphasizes calibrated redefinition, not abandonment.
- **Aware of survivorship bias.** Pathless paths are more visible when they succeed; failures are silent.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling + builder PM emergence** has expanded "founder-track" options. Cf. [becoming-senior-pm](becoming-senior-pm.md): 14 of 125 in Singhal's community founded companies in the last year.
- **Career uncertainty drives more redefinition conversations.** When the "stable big-tech career" assumption decays, people examine alternatives more readily.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a senior IC questioning your trajectory**: the corpus has multiple guides. Paul Millerd, Graham Weaver, Jerry Colonna, Chip Conley.
- **If you're a manager noticing a high performer disengaging**: career redefinition is a common cause. Real conversation > performance management.
- **If you're an exec planning org for the next 5 years**: top talent will quit for non-financial reasons in 2026 more often than in 2018. Compensation alone is insufficient retention.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2024**: Paul Millerd, Graham Weaver, Jerry Colonna episodes on career redefinition.
- **2025-2026**: Builder-track / founder-track alternative legitimization (Singhal).
