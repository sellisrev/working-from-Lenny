# Shipping fast like a startup

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "How to ship like a startup" (2024) + Geoff Charles ("Velocity over everything: How Ramp became the fastest-growing SaaS startup of all time", 2024) + Cat Wu (Anthropic Claude Code, 2026).

### The core operating principles
1. **Ship in days, not months.** Most teams over-deliberate. Most decisions reverse cheaply.
2. **Research preview > formal launch.** Ship something rough; brand it as preview; iterate on real feedback.
3. **Reduce coordination overhead.** Fewer review steps, fewer approvals, fewer status meetings.
4. **High trust + clear standards.** Speed without standards produces mess; standards without trust produce paralysis.
5. **Cut scope ruthlessly.** Most "shipping fast" failures are scope-creep issues, not engineering issues.

### Geoff Charles / Ramp specifics
- **Velocity is a culture, not a process.** Hiring, onboarding, decision rights all reinforce velocity.
- **Speed is a recruiting magnet.** Top builders want to ship; slow companies lose them.
- **Velocity at scale.** Ramp scaled velocity past $300M ARR; not just an early-stage thing.

### Cat Wu / Anthropic Claude Code (2026)
- **Research preview as primary shipping vehicle.** Features ship in 1-2 weeks, branded as preview, iterated on user feedback.
- **PRDs are lightweight.** Goal + user + top use cases. Not 30-page specs.
- **Engineers ship end-to-end.** Many features at Anthropic ship without dedicated PM involvement.

### Common failure modes
- **"Ship fast" as cover for "ship sloppy".** Speed without quality is worse than slow with quality.
- **Reverse the velocity-trust order.** Demanding speed without earning trust produces compliance, not throughput.
- **Excluding non-engineering from velocity.** PMs, designers, support — all need to operate at the team's pace.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling 2x's throughput** at well-instrumented companies. Cf. Brian Scanlan / Intercom (cf. [ai-developer-productivity](ai-developer-productivity.md)).
- **The bottleneck has shifted to judgment.** Speed of execution is no longer the limiter; speed of good decisions is.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CTO / VP Eng**: process drag is your enemy. Audit every approval / review / meeting; cut what doesn't earn its keep.
- **If you're a CEO**: velocity-as-culture is one of the most defensible moats. Cf. [seven-powers](seven-powers.md) — process power.
- **If you're a designer or PM**: don't be the bottleneck. Ship your prototypes; review on async cadence; don't gate engineering on weekly review meetings.
