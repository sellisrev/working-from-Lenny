# What it takes to be a top 1% PM

## Current consensus

The corpus has an evolving but consistent view of what separates the best PMs from competent ones. Anchored in:
- **Ian McAllister** ("What it takes to become a top 1% PM", 2022)
- **14 habits of highly effective product managers** (Lenny, 2021)
- **Cat Wu / Anthropic and Nikhyl Singhal v2** (2026 updates)

### Core attributes (durable across the corpus)
1. **Customer obsession that's not performative.** Talks to users multiple times per week; quotes specific customers in conversations.
2. **Crisp judgment.** Picks the right thing to ship more often than peers; can articulate why.
3. **Strong opinions, weakly held.** Conviction without rigidity. Mihika Kapoor framing.
4. **Bias to action.** Ships, iterates, learns. Not "more meetings, more analysis."
5. **Cross-functional credibility.** Engineers and designers want to work with them.
6. **Strategic + tactical.** Can zoom in to detail and zoom out to strategy without strain.
7. **Owns outcomes.** No deflecting blame; no hoarding credit.
8. **Continuous learning loop.** Reads, talks to peers, updates priors visibly.
9. **Storytelling.** Can make a complex strategic case in 5 minutes to anyone.
10. **Operates at next level.** Top 1% PMs operate one level above their title for years before promotion catches up.

### The 2026 additions (AI era)
11. **AI fluency at the practitioner level.** Uses Cursor / Claude Code daily; ships prototypes.
12. **Eval intuition.** Cf. [evals-for-ai-products](evals-for-ai-products.md).
13. **AGI-calibration.** Can be the right amount of AGI-pilled (Cat Wu).
14. **Builder, not coordinator.** Cf. [ai-pm-skills](ai-pm-skills.md).

### What top 1% PMs do not do
- Hide behind frameworks.
- Confuse activity with progress.
- Win arguments by escalation.
- Take credit for the team's work.
- Pretend confidence they don't have.

### How they got there
- **Mentorship.** Most top PMs cite a 2-3 person mentor circle; reciprocal over years.
- **Volume of decisions.** Top PMs have made many calls; their judgment is calibrated by experience.
- **Cross-functional roots.** Many top 1% PMs came from engineering, design, or operations originally.
- **Sustained focus.** 5-10 years on the same product / domain produces deeper insight than 1 year × 5 different domains.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a hiring manager evaluating senior PM candidates**: probe for these traits, not for canned answers. Ask "tell me about a customer-driven decision that surprised you."
- **If you're an engineer working with a PM you suspect is top-tier**: pay attention; their patterns are learnable.
- **If you're a CEO with one top 1% PM on the team**: their compensation should reflect that they're rare. Underpaying them is the most common reason they leave.

## How thinking has evolved
