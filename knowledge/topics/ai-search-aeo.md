# AI search and answer-engine optimization (AEO)

## Current consensus

Search has bifurcated into two coexisting modes — and the SEO playbook of 2020-2024 only fully covers one of them.

1. **Traditional Google search** — still huge, but the click-through pattern has changed. AI Overviews + AI Mode now consume the queries where the user wants an answer (not a navigation), so the "win the long-form post and get the first click" play has degraded for those queries. ([Eli Schwartz, 2024-09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z71yGshPTwk))

2. **Answer engines** (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, AI Mode, AI Overviews) — the user asks a question and gets a synthesized answer with maybe-cited sources. This is **AEO: Answer Engine Optimization** — getting your product / brand / content to be the source the answer engine cites or recommends. ([Ethan Smith, 2025-09-14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iT7kq-R3Gjc))

The 2026 working consensus:

- **Google's slice of the pie stays the same; the pie gets bigger.** Ethan Smith's framing. AI search adds query volume rather than fully cannibalizing Google. Robby Stein (Google VP Product Search, 2025-10) confirms from the inside: AI Overviews and AI Mode are growing the addressable search market.
- **AEO uses different signals than SEO.** Authority and citation patterns matter more; backlinks and "exact-match keyword in title" matter less. Branded mentions in authoritative content (where you're cited as a source) are the new high-value link.
- **The Eli Schwartz "apocalypse" framing was wrong.** Schwartz himself opens his episode admitting "I thought this was going to be an apocalypse." It hasn't been. Direct organic traffic to long-form content has decreased for some query types but content quality + brand recognition still win in answer engines.
- **Don't write for AI; write for the audience the AI is summarizing for.** The shortcut tactic of "include schema for AI" or "write a paragraph optimized for excerpting" has not consistently outperformed quality content.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a marketing lead**: rebudget. Less spent on long-tail keyword content factories; more spent on (a) earning authoritative citations / press / industry reports, (b) brand mentions in answer-engine training/grounding data, (c) direct properties (newsletter, podcast, community) the answer engine has to cite as a source.
- **If you're a CMO at a B2B company**: AEO favors brands that are genuinely the authority in their category. Category-creation moves (cf. [category-creation](category-creation.md)) get bigger payoffs in AEO than in SEO because answer engines have a winner-take-most cite distribution.
- **If you're a content team or freelancer**: writing-for-LLM-training is unstable; LLMs are trained on snapshots and your content's lifetime value as training data is short. Write for humans who'll cite you, and let the LLMs harvest from there.
- **If you're a CEO setting growth targets**: don't budget for SEO traffic to grow at historical rates over the next 18 months. Plan a transition where SEO traffic stabilizes or declines while AEO-driven brand traffic grows. The blended traffic should be flat-to-up if you execute, but the channel mix changes substantially.

## How thinking has evolved

Earliest takes (corpus, oldest first):

- **2020-07-14**: Brian Ta (Lenny newsletter) — "Winning at SEO." Standard pre-AI SEO playbook: keyword research, link building, content factory, technical SEO. Worked beautifully for Airbnb, Strava, AngelList. ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/winning-at-seo))

- **2024-06-11**: Lenny — "How to use Perplexity in your PM work" (newsletter). First broad-audience corpus piece treating answer engines as a research tool, not just a search alternative.

Middle period:

- **2024-09-19**: Eli Schwartz — "Rethinking SEO in the age of AI." First major corpus piece naming the shift. Schwartz himself says "I thought this was going to be an apocalypse"; opens the conversation that the shift is real but not destruction. AI Overviews launch documented at Google I/O 2024 with the famous "Google told me to put glue on my pizza" early failures. ([Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z71yGshPTwk))

Recent takes:

- **2025-09-14**: Ethan Smith (Graphite) — "The ultimate guide to AEO." Frames AEO as a new discipline; gives concrete tactics. Claims "Google's slice of the pie stays the same. The pie gets bigger" — counter-narrative to AI-killing-Google framing. ([Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iT7kq-R3Gjc))

- **2025-10-10**: Robby Stein (Google VP Product Search) — confirms from inside Google: AI Mode + AI Overviews are growing search volume, not cannibalizing it. Google Gemini hit #1 in App Store the prior week. Re-framing: Google was slow but is now competitive in the AI search arena.

External current consensus (web, weighted recent + AI-aware):

- AI search market reports from late 2025 / early 2026 (Sparktoro, Similarweb, Ahrefs) confirm the bifurcation: traditional Google traffic is mostly stable for navigational queries; informational queries are losing click-through rate.
- ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity now consistently cite sources in answers. Inclusion in those citations is increasingly tracked as a marketing KPI.

## Inflection points

- **AI Overviews launch (May 2024)**: Google embeds AI summaries above the blue links for many queries. Click-through rate to publishers drops; the "first blue link" SEO outcome stops being the win.
- **ChatGPT search release (late 2024)**: ChatGPT becomes a viable Google substitute for many information queries.
- **Eli Schwartz episode (Sept 2024)**: corpus-formal acknowledgement that SEO doctrine is shifting.
- **Ethan Smith AEO episode (Sept 2025)**: corpus introduces AEO as its own discipline.
- **Google AI Mode launch (2025)** + **Gemini hits #1 (Oct 2025)**: Google demonstrates it can compete head-on in AI-native search. Reframes the "Google is dead" narrative.

## Open questions

- What's the long-term economic structure for content publishers when AI answers replace clicks? The corpus has not addressed this directly, beyond Schwartz's pragmatic "the apocalypse didn't happen."
- How do answer engines handle source attribution at scale? Litigation and licensing deals are emerging but pre-consensus.
- For e-commerce specifically: does AEO favor large brands with broader citation footprints, or can niche / new entrants win answer-engine recommendations? Ethan Smith's tactical advice suggests the latter is possible but harder than legacy SEO.
- Does multi-modal AI search (visual + voice) reshape AEO again? Robby Stein hints yes; corpus has not yet covered this.
