# Feature-team PMs (defense and discontent)

## Current consensus

"Feature team" PM (vs. empowered / outcome team PM) is a recognized organizational pattern. The corpus has competing views, anchored in:
- **Marty Cagan / SVPG canon**: feature teams are a pathology; empowered teams are the goal.
- **Lenny "In defense of feature team product managers" (2024)**: the pattern is more nuanced; feature teams have legitimate roles in some contexts.

### The Cagan / empowered-team model
- Teams given problems to solve, not features to build.
- Outcome accountability.
- Cross-functional, durable team membership.
- The PM as keeper of vision and strategy.

### The Lenny "in defense" framing
Some companies legitimately structure as feature teams, especially:
- **Very large enterprises** with cross-team coordination needs that empowered teams can't handle alone.
- **Heavily regulated industries** where every change must trace to a documented requirement.
- **Service-and-support-driven products** where roadmap is reactive to enterprise contracts.
- **Pre-PMF startups** where founder-led product strategy is correct, not a delegation gap.

The Cagan critique is right that *most* feature teams are pathologies. Lenny's defense: not *all* of them are.

### How to tell the difference
- **Pathological feature team**: PM is order-taker; team has no input on priority; metrics not connected to user value.
- **Legitimate feature team**: clear strategic context above the team; team owns *how* but not *what*; outcomes still tracked.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-tooling reduces the case for feature teams.** When small teams can ship cross-functionally, the coordination needs that justified feature teams shrink. Cf. [ai-org-restructuring](ai-org-restructuring.md), [ai-pm-skills](ai-pm-skills.md).
- **Singhal "information mover dinosaur"** framing pattern-matches feature-team PM. The career signal in 2026 is to escape feature-team PM roles.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineer working in a feature team**: you may be right that the team structure is suboptimal. Read both Cagan (empowered) and Lenny ("in defense") to calibrate.
- **If you're a CEO running feature teams**: ask honestly whether you're choosing it for legitimate reasons (regulated industry, etc.) or because changing is uncomfortable. The pattern is usually the latter.
- **If you're a PM in a feature team and unhappy**: career-wise, transition to an empowered-team org. Singhal's bifurcation suggests information-mover archetype is shed; builder PM is the durable role.

## How thinking has evolved

- **Pre-corpus**: Cagan's canonical empowered-team vs feature-team distinction.
- **2024**: Lenny "In defense of feature team product managers" — corpus first defends the pattern.
- **2025-2026**: AI-era undermines remaining cases for feature teams in most contexts.
