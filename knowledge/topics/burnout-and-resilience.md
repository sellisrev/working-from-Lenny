# Burnout, resilience, and tech worker wellbeing

## Current consensus

The corpus consensus, anchored in Jonny Miller (*Nervous System Mastery*, 2024), Hilary Gridley ("playbook for building resilient teams", 2024-2025), and Lenny "How tech's most resilient workers handle burnout" (2026):

### What works
1. **Recovery is a system, not willpower.** Scheduled sleep, scheduled exercise, scheduled disengagement. People who "just push through" eventually break.
2. **Nervous system regulation matters.** Jonny Miller's framing: chronic stress dysregulates the nervous system; deliberate practices (breath work, somatic, mindfulness) restore it.
3. **Team-level resilience over individual heroics.** Hilary Gridley: build a team that can "take a punch" — distributed coping, no single point of failure.
4. **Mental models for stress.** Annie Duke (decision-making under uncertainty), Carole Robin (relationships), Joe Hudson (emotions as signal) — multiple corpus voices on healthy stress responses.
5. **Disengagement on schedule.** Tech's most resilient workers don't work weekends; they don't check Slack in the evenings; they vacation fully. The corpus consensus is unanimous against the always-on archetype.

### What doesn't work
- **"No days off" as a generalized prescription.** Cf. [`../cautions/ai-replacing-pms.md`](../cautions/ai-replacing-pms.md). Survivorship bias from people with calendar control + accumulated capital.
- **Performative self-care.** Posting wellness content while still working 80-hour weeks is theater, not recovery.
- **Hustle culture as identity.** Self-conception as "the person who works hardest" rationalizes burnout.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling reduces some burnout-driving work** (status updates, repetitive tasks). Gains often re-spent on more ambitious work; net wellbeing impact is unclear.
- **AI work makes time-off more valuable.** Multiple corpus guests (Brian Scanlan, Cat Wu) report the post-Christmas-break inflection — engineers who took time off came back transformed by the AI exploration time. Argues for *more* PTO, not less.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a manager whose team is burning out**: scheduled disengagement is your lever. Cancel weekend pings; enforce 1 week unbroken vacation per quarter.
- **If you're an exec watching attrition**: burnout-driven attrition is expensive and largely preventable.
- **If you're an individual hitting burnout**: see Jonny Miller's Nervous System Mastery course / podcast appearance for tactical recovery. Start with sleep.
- **If you're a CFO**: PTO usage is a leading indicator of org health. Teams with low PTO usage are usually struggling, not high-performing.
