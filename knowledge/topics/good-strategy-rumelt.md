# Good Strategy / Bad Strategy (Rumelt)

## Current consensus

Richard Rumelt's *Good Strategy / Bad Strategy* is the corpus-canonical reference for distinguishing real strategy from fluff. Cf. book digest [`../books/good-strategy-bad-strategy.md`](../books/good-strategy-bad-strategy.md). This topic file is for cross-referencing the key principles when the framework is invoked from elsewhere.

### The kernel of strategy (Rumelt)
1. **Diagnosis**: what's actually going on? Causal model of the situation.
2. **Guiding policy**: what we'll do about it. Direction, not detail.
3. **Coherent action**: concrete moves that fit together. Mutually reinforcing.

### Bad strategy = "fluff"
- Empty slogans ("be the best in class")
- Motherhood goals ("delight customers")
- Aspirational statements masquerading as analysis
- Multiple-priority lists where everything is priority

### When to invoke this framework
- **Reviewing a strategy doc.** Apply the kernel as a diagnostic.
- **Distinguishing strategy from goals.** Goals describe desired outcomes; strategy explains how.
- **Cutting fluff.** A strategy that doesn't survive removing every aspirational sentence is mostly fluff.

### Rumelt's "saying no" insight
- **Good strategy is fundamentally about saying no.** Choosing what NOT to do.
- The discomfort of saying no is why bad strategy persists.

### When the framework has limits
- **For very early-stage startups**: pre-PMF strategy is closer to "validate this hypothesis" than "diagnose + policy + action."
- **In rapidly-shifting markets** (AI 2024-2026): "coherent action" cycles compress; you may need to reform strategy more often than Rumelt's framework implies.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-era strategy compression.** "Coherent action" cycles measured in weeks, not quarters.
- **Distribution-as-moat reframing** (Spiegel) provides a specific concrete-action category.
- **Cf. [assessing-product-strategy-as-board](assessing-product-strategy-as-board.md), [evaluating-product-bets](evaluating-product-bets.md)**: Rumelt + Roger Martin 5-questions + Helmer 7 Powers stack as the corpus's canonical strategy-diagnostic toolkit.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an exec reviewing strategy docs**: apply the kernel diagnostic. Most strategy presentations are fluff.
- **If you're a board member**: ask "what's the diagnosis?" If the answer is generic, the rest of the strategy is unmoored.
- **If you're a CEO writing a strategy**: write the diagnosis first; if it's vague or aspirational, fix that before moving to policy and actions.
