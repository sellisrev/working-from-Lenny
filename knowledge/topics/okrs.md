# OKRs and goal-setting

## Current consensus

Anchored in Christina Wodtke ("The ultimate guide to OKRs", 2023) + Lenny "Setting goals" canon (2020-2021).

### What OKRs are
- **Objective**: qualitative, ambitious goal. ("Become the dominant scheduling product for SMB.")
- **Key Results**: 3-5 measurable outcomes that, if achieved, mean the Objective is met.
- **Cycle**: typically quarterly or trimester; some companies use annual.

### Why OKRs work (when they work)
- **Forcing function for clarity.** Vague intent gets exposed when you have to write a measurable KR.
- **Alignment across teams.** Shared OKR vocabulary makes cross-team coordination cheaper.
- **Continuous reflection.** Quarterly reviews force learning loops.

### Why OKRs fail (when they fail)
- **OKRs as performance management.** Tying personal comp to OKR completion produces sandbagging.
- **Cascaded OKRs.** Top-down OKRs cascaded to teams produce reactive teams, not strategic ones.
- **Too many KRs per Objective.** 3-5 is the limit; 10 means the team isn't actually focused.
- **OKRs as theater.** Performative OKR cycles without real review produce no value.

### Christina Wodtke's specifics
- **OKRs are stretch goals.** 70% completion is healthy; 100% means the goals were too easy.
- **Weekly check-ins matter more than the doc.** The doc creates the goal; weekly cadence drives execution.
- **OKRs and CFRs (conversations, feedback, recognition) together.** Doerr's framing.

### When NOT to use OKRs
- **Pre-PMF.** Goal cycles are too long for pre-PMF iteration speed.
- **Very small teams.** 5-person teams don't need formal goal-setting infrastructure.
- **Highly reactive contexts.** Customer-success, support, ops sometimes operate on shorter loops than OKRs handle.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-era cycle compression.** Some AI-native companies (Linear, Cursor, Anthropic) use lighter goal-setting because shipping cadence is weekly, not quarterly.
- **Cf. [shape-up-method](shape-up-method.md), [defending-big-bets](defending-big-bets.md)**: alternative frameworks that some teams prefer.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO at a small company**: don't impose enterprise-OKR ceremony on a startup. Lighter goal-setting often works better.
- **If you're a manager**: OKR completion isn't your team's evaluation. Use OKRs for direction; evaluate people on judgment + impact.
- **If you're an exec frustrated by your OKRs**: probably the cascade pattern. Try empowered teams setting their own KRs against shared Objectives.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2020-2021**: Lenny "Setting goals" canon; OKRs become widely-adopted vocabulary.
- **2023-12**: Christina Wodtke on Lenny's Podcast — corpus-canonical reference.
- **2025-2026**: AI-native companies move toward lighter frameworks; OKRs still dominant at scale.
