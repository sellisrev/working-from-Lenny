# Why marketplaces fail

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "Why marketplaces fail" (2021) + Andrew Chen *The Cold Start Problem* + Sarah Tavel hierarchy of marketplaces.

### Top failure modes (Lenny canonical)
1. **Cold start never resolved.** Without enough supply OR enough demand, the marketplace doesn't reach liquidity.
2. **Wrong side prioritized first.** Most consumer marketplaces are supply-constrained; pre-selling demand without supply burns capital.
3. **Trust failures.** Bad transactions destroy demand-side perception; one viral horror story can kill a marketplace.
4. **Take rate misalignment.** Cf. [take-rate](take-rate.md). Too high alienates supply; too low starves the platform.
5. **Disintermediation.** Suppliers + buyers transact off-platform once trust is established.
6. **Lack of repeat use.** One-time-use marketplaces struggle vs frequent-use ones (the LTV : CAC math is brutal).
7. **Geographic / category fragmentation issues.** Marketplaces that need density per geography or category fail when they spread thin.

### What separates successful marketplaces from failed ones
- **Frequent transactions.** Daily / weekly use beats annual.
- **High-trust supply.** Curation or verification beats open-listing.
- **Network effects compound.** Each new user makes the platform more valuable.
- **Non-marketplace fundamentals.** Sarah Tavel's hierarchy: many marketplaces fail because of generic startup reasons (PMF, team, market) before marketplace-specific reasons.

### The Andrew Chen Cold Start Problem framing
- **Most marketplaces die in the cold start.** Atomic networks (smallest viable network unit) need to be solved one by one.
- **The most-failed pattern is "let's launch nationally on day 1".** Cold-start one geography, prove it, expand.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-mediated matching** can compress cold-start timelines but doesn't eliminate the problem.
- **Eval marketplaces** (Mercor) demonstrate new marketplace categories arising from AI demand.
- **Distribution as moat** (Spiegel) — marketplaces with unique distribution have more durable cold-start advantages.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a marketplace founder**: study failures more than successes. Why marketplaces died is more transferable knowledge than why specific ones succeeded.
- **If you're an investor evaluating a marketplace**: ask about the cold-start plan; ask about repeat-use math; ask about disintermediation defenses.
- **If you're an existing operator considering a marketplace pivot**: marketplaces are usually the hardest path to take. Calibrate carefully.
