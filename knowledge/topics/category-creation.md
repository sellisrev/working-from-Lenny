# Category creation and category pirates

## Current consensus

Anchored in Christopher Lochhead (*Play Bigger / Niche Down / Category Pirates*, 2023 episode) + Barbra Gago (Pando, "Category creation and brand building") + Kevin Yien on naming and positioning.

### What category creation is
- **Defining a new market category** rather than competing in an existing one.
- **Naming the category.** "CRM" started as one company's framing. "Marketing automation," "PLG," "AI evals" — each was named into existence.
- **Owning the category.** Most categories produce one dominant winner and 2-3 followers.

### Why category creation works
- **Categories shape buyer cognition.** Once a category is established, buyers shop within it.
- **Defining the category lets you set the criteria.** Whoever names the category usually wins it.
- **Exit value scales with category dominance.** Category-king status produces $10B+ outcomes; runner-up positions are 10-100x less valuable.

### The Lochhead playbook
1. **Identify a "category fault line"** — a problem the existing categories don't solve well.
2. **Name and frame the new category.** Specific words; consistent usage.
3. **Build a movement, not a product.** Conferences, books, community.
4. **Educate the market.** Buyers don't know they need this; teach them.
5. **Establish category dominance.** Visible market share, thought leadership, customer evangelism.

### When category creation is wrong
- **The market already has a working category.** You're better off as a fast follower with better product.
- **You don't have the resources to invest in market education.** Category creation is multi-year, multi-million-dollar.
- **Your insight isn't pattern-breaking** (cf. [pattern-breakers](pattern-breakers.md)). Without a real inflection-driven insight, "creating a category" is positioning theater.

### Common failure modes
- **Calling yourself a category** without buyer adoption of the term. Saying "we created the X category" when buyers don't recognize the term is delusion.
- **Multi-category positioning.** "We're the leader in X AND Y AND Z" tells buyers nothing.
- **Underinvesting in education.** Categories require teaching the market; without teaching, the category never solidifies.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI is producing many new categories** (AI eval marketplaces, AI coding agents, vibe-coding tools, etc.).
- **Distribution determines category outcomes** (cf. [distribution-as-moat](distribution-as-moat.md)). Whoever has the distribution wins the AI category.
- **Lochhead's 2024 framework** still durable but compressed timelines for AI categories.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CMO / brand lead**: category creation is your highest-leverage move. It's also expensive.
- **If you're a CEO contemplating category creation**: budget 3-5 years and significant capital. Halfway invested often yields nothing.
- **If you're an investor**: category-king bets pay disproportionately. Category-runner-up bets often disappoint.
