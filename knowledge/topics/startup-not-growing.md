# What to do when your product stops growing

## Current consensus

Anchored in Jason Cohen ("5 questions to ask when your product stops growing", 2026-01) + Lenny "What to do if your product isn't taking off" (2024).

### Jason Cohen's 5 questions
1. **Logo retention.** Are existing customers staying? If not, you have retention not growth problem.
2. **Pricing.** Is your pricing aligned with delivered value? Misaligned pricing strangles growth.
3. **Net dollar retention (NDR).** Are existing customers expanding? Healthy NDR (>110%) compounds.
4. **Marketing channels.** Have your acquisition channels saturated? New channels needed?
5. **Target market.** Is your ICP shifting / has the market changed under you?

### The diagnostic order matters
- **Start with retention** (logo + NDR). Without retention, growth is a leaking bucket.
- **Then pricing.** Often the underrated lever.
- **Then channels.** Channel saturation is the most-cited "growth slowdown" cause but often masks deeper retention/pricing issues.
- **Finally market.** Pivots/ICP shifts are last-resort answers; other questions usually surface first.

### Lenny's complementary framing (2024)
- **Cohort analysis first.** Is the issue affecting all cohorts or just new ones?
- **Talk to churned customers.** Why they left often diagnoses the issue.
- **Talk to power users.** What's working tells you what to scale.
- **Audit recent product changes.** Sometimes growth slowdown traces to a specific shipped feature.

### Common patterns
- **PMF was for Segment A; you're now selling to Segment B.** Cf. [icp-identification](icp-identification.md). Mid-stage growth slowdowns often reflect ICP drift.
- **Channel saturation.** First-channel marketing eventually exhausts; need new channels.
- **Product fatigue.** Same product for 3+ years without major feature work loses momentum.
- **Competitive entry.** Cf. [crossing-the-chasm](crossing-the-chasm.md). New competitor in the segment changes dynamics.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-feature additions** are sometimes the cure (genuinely new value), sometimes the diversion (chasing AI without core-product fit).
- **AEO + answer-engine shifts** have caused traffic-driven growth slowdowns at content-driven products. Cf. [seo-strategy](seo-strategy.md).

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO seeing growth slow**: do Jason Cohen's diagnostic before reacting. Right diagnosis vs. wrong fix is the difference.
- **If you're a board member**: growth slowdowns are predictable; stable product + same channels for too long = saturation. Ask about the diagnosis and the response, not just the metrics.
- **If you're an investor**: growth slowdown isn't death; misdiagnosis is. Companies that diagnose well often re-accelerate.
