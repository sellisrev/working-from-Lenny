# Validating a startup idea

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "How to validate your startup idea" (2022) + "How to validate your B2B startup idea" (2023) + Mike Maples Jr. (*Pattern Breakers*, 2024).

### The core practice
1. **Talk to potential customers before you build.** Most failed startups skipped this; most successful ones did it obsessively.
2. **Look for inflection-driven opportunities.** Mike Maples: the best startup ideas ride a market inflection (technology shift, regulatory change, behavior change). Without an inflection, you're competing in a stable market — much harder.
3. **The Mom Test rules.** Rob Fitzpatrick's *Mom Test* canon: don't ask "would you use this?" Ask about past behavior, current pain, and what they've already tried.
4. **Money is the truth-teller.** Pre-orders, paid LOIs, deposit-with-refund-promise — get someone to put money down before you commit to building.
5. **Iterate the problem framing, not just the solution.** Most validation work reveals you have the wrong problem framing.

### Lenny's B2B-specific addition (2023)
- **Find 3-5 design partners** who'll work with you for a year on a discount-with-feedback arrangement.
- **The first deal teaches you most.** Subsequent deals confirm or refute the lessons.
- **Founder-led sales until you have a working playbook.** Cf. [b2b-first-customers](b2b-first-customers.md), [founder-led-sales](founder-led-sales.md).

### Common failure modes
- **Asking leading questions.** "Would you find this useful?" gets polite yes; tells you nothing.
- **Confusing interest with intent.** "I'd love to try it" is interest; "I'll pay for it on day 1" is intent.
- **Solving founder problems instead of customer problems.** Cf. Mike Maples on "your enemy is general malaise."
- **Pre-product over-investment.** Building before validating wastes time.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI prototyping compresses validation cycles.** A vibe-coded prototype in 30 minutes is a more powerful validation tool than a deck.
- **The "live demo" is now the validation interview standard.** Bring a working prototype; iterate live.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a founder pre-PMF**: spend 80% of your time talking to customers, 20% building. Most reverse this and fail.
- **If you're an investor evaluating a Pre-Seed pitch**: look for evidence the founder has done validation work. "I think customers would want this" is weak; "I have 12 LOIs and 3 paid pilots" is strong.
- **If you're a non-founder evaluating "should I quit my job and build X"**: do the validation BEFORE you quit.
