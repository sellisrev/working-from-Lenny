# Naming products and brands

## Current consensus

David Placek (Lexicon Branding — "Naming expert shares the process behind creating billion-dollar brand names", 2025-06) is the corpus-canonical recent treatment. Reinforced by Lenny "How to find the perfect name" (2024) and Arielle Jackson on brand.

### David Placek's process (creator of Pentium, BlackBerry, Vercel, Windsurf, Sonos, Azure, Impossible Burger)
1. **Three teams, different briefs.** Run multiple parallel naming exercises with subtly different framings to surface different name spaces.
2. **You won't know a great name when you see it.** Initial discomfort is often the signal of a great name. Brain hates novelty.
3. **8 weeks for a real naming process.** Less is rushed; more is over-engineering.
4. **Avoid big team brainstorms.** They produce safe names. Small expert teams produce distinctive names.
5. **Letters have feelings.** Specific letters and sounds carry connotations. Worth understanding.

### What makes a great name
- **Distinctive.** Doesn't sound like the category.
- **Easy to say and spell.** Audio test: people understand and remember without seeing it written.
- **Domain available.** Practical reality.
- **Not legally constrained.** Trademark clearance is essential.
- **Future-proof.** Doesn't lock you into a specific category as you expand.

### Lenny "How to find the perfect name" (2024)
- **Most product names are over-thought OR under-thought.** Spend the right amount of time, not infinite or zero.
- **Test names with target audience, not personal network.**
- **Let the founder's gut have weight.** The founder will live with the name; their conviction matters.

### Common failure modes
- **Generic descriptive names.** "AI Sales Tool" is a category, not a name.
- **Cute / clever names that don't scale.** Easy to mock; hard to trademark.
- **Founders falling in love with bad names early.** Once committed, hard to change.
- **Skipping legal clearance.** Discovering trademark conflict after launch is expensive.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling generates many name candidates fast.** Useful for option generation; doesn't replace judgment.
- **AI-era names** (Lovable, Cursor, Anthropic, Perplexity, Gemini) tend toward evocative single words.
- **AEO matters.** Distinctive names rank better in answer engines than generic descriptive names.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a founder naming a startup**: give it real time. 1-2 weekends rarely produces a great name.
- **If you're a CEO renaming a product line**: read Placek; budget 8 weeks of focused work.
- **If you're an engineer or designer**: trust the marketing / brand team's judgment on names. Names that sound bad to engineers often work fine for customers.
