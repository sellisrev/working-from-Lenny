# First 90 days as a PM (or in any new role)

## Current consensus

The corpus consensus, anchored in Lenny's "How to make an impact in your first 90 days" (2024-04):

### The 30/60/90 framework
- **Days 1-30**: Listen. Meet everyone. Ask the same question 50 different ways. Don't propose changes. Build the mental model.
- **Days 31-60**: Test hypotheses. Run small experiments. Form opinions. Surface to your manager.
- **Days 61-90**: Make a meaningful change visible to leadership. Ship something or shift something. Establish credibility.

### The traps
- **Hero mode early.** Coming in with "I'll fix everything" before understanding the context burns trust quickly.
- **Pure-listening mode forever.** Going past 90 days without showing impact also burns credibility.
- **Replicating your last company.** What worked at Stripe doesn't necessarily work at your new place.
- **Skipping skip-level relationships.** Build them in week 1-2; harder to establish later.

### What works
- **Document everything in your first 90 days.** Customer interviews, team conversations, decision rationales. This is data you can't easily recreate later.
- **Ask "what should I stop doing?" of your peers.** Reveals process baggage you'd otherwise inherit.
- **Find your "first win."** A scoped, achievable, visible delivery in days 60-90.
- **Pair with a long-tenure colleague.** Their context-pattern recognition is highest-leverage gift you'll get.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling lets you ship faster in your first 90.** A vibe-coded prototype demo in week 4 establishes credibility that took 90 days pre-AI.
- **For AI-product roles**: building eval intuition in your first 30 is now table-stakes. Cf. [evals-for-ai-products](evals-for-ai-products.md).

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a hiring manager onboarding a new PM**: structure a 30/60/90 plan together in their first week. Reduces ambiguity dramatically.
- **If you're a senior IC**: you'll be a key relationship for any new PM. Be generous with context; expect to repeat yourself.
- **If you're a CEO bringing in a senior product hire**: the "first 100 days" is real. Push back on "I want to assess for 6 months before changing anything" — that's too long.
- **If you're a non-PM in a new role**: same playbook applies broadly across roles.
