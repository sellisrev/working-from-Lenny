# Kickstarting and scaling a consumer business

## Current consensus

Lenny's six-part series "How to kickstart and scale a consumer business" (2020-2021) is the corpus-canonical playbook.

### The six stages
1. **Kickstart**: validate the idea with a tight first audience.
2. **Identify your super-specific who**: narrow segment definition; resist generality.
3. **Craft your pitch**: what problem you solve and for whom.
4. **Find your early adopters by doing things that don't scale**: founder-led concierge work.
5. **Retain — iterate until enough people stick around**: PMF check via cohort retention.
6. **Scale — build your growth engine**: only after PMF; only after retention is solid.

### Why this sequencing matters
- **Premature scaling kills consumer startups.** Most failed consumer products spent paid acquisition before solving retention.
- **The "super-specific who" is the hardest step.** Founders resist narrowing; the resistance is usually wrong.
- **Things-that-don't-scale produces signal scaled marketing can't.** Direct conversations with early adopters reveal the product.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-native consumer products** (Lovable, Cursor for non-engineers) compress this timeline. The 6 stages still apply; the duration shrinks.
- **AI-generated viral artifacts** (cf. [virality](virality.md)) are a new acquisition primitive at stage 4.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a consumer startup founder**: don't skip stages. Most consumer-startup failures skipped the "super-specific who" or the "do things that don't scale."
- **If you're an investor evaluating a consumer pitch**: ask which stage they're in honestly. Stage-1 founders pitching as stage-6 are a red flag.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2020-2021**: Lenny six-part series — corpus-foundational consumer canon.
- **2022-2024**: Saturn case study, Nikita Bier playbook update the virality piece.
- **2025-2026**: AI-native consumer compression; stages durable, durations shorter.
