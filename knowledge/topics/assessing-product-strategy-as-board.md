# Assessing product strategy as an exec / board / investor

Cf. [`evaluating-product-bets`](evaluating-product-bets.md) for evaluating individual bets. This topic is for assessing the *overall* product strategy — at a board meeting, a portfolio review, or an exec review.

## Current consensus

The corpus consensus has settled on a small number of high-leverage frameworks. Use them in sequence, not in parallel.

### Frame 1: Roger Martin's 5 questions (canonical strategy diagnostic)

Same questions as [evaluating-product-bets](evaluating-product-bets.md): winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities, management systems. For overall strategy assessment, all five must have crisp answers. Strategy that can't answer all five is not strategy; it's aspiration.

### Frame 2: 7 Powers (Hamilton Helmer, [Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SaiQfLIVabQ))

A concrete moat taxonomy. The 7 powers:
- **Scale economies**, **Network effects**, **Counter-positioning**, **Switching costs**, **Branding**, **Cornered resource**, **Process power**.

For board / investor review: which of these does the company have, building, or aspiring to? "Better product" is not a power. If the company can't articulate which of the 7 powers they're building, they don't have strategy clarity.

### Frame 3: Good Strategy / Bad Strategy (Richard Rumelt)

Good strategy has three elements: **diagnosis** (what's going on), **guiding policy** (what we'll do about it), **coherent action** (concrete moves that fit together). Bad strategy is full of "fluff" (motherhood goals, slogans masquerading as analysis). If a strategy doc is mostly aspirational language, that's the diagnostic.

### Frame 4: Pre-mortem (Annie Duke / Thinking in Bets)

"Imagine it's 18 months from now and the strategy failed. What killed it?" The answers reveal load-bearing assumptions. A team that can't generate plausible failure modes hasn't internalized risk.

### Frame 5: Distribution as primary moat (Evan Spiegel, 2026; Aparna Chennapragada)

A 2026 corpus addition. "Distribution has become the most important moat" — Evan Spiegel. For AI-era strategy review, ask: what's the distribution advantage? Software is no longer a moat (per various 2026 framings); distribution increasingly is.

### Frame 6: AI-readiness assessment (2026 update)

For any strategy reviewed in 2026, additional questions:
- Is the strategy built for an AI-native competitor entering the market? Or is it a pre-AI strategy with AI features bolted on?
- Howie Liu's question: "If we were founding from scratch today AI-native, would we execute this way?" The gap between the answer and the current strategy is the strategy's AI-debt.

## What this means for execs / board / investors specifically

- **If you're a board member at a portfolio company**: ask all 5 of Roger Martin's questions explicitly at every board meeting. If answers haven't sharpened in 6 months, the team isn't doing strategy work.
- **If you're an investor evaluating a Series B+ company**: the 7 Powers diagnostic separates real moats from feature stories. A company that can't name their power is buying the next round of fundraising on momentum, not durability.
- **If you're a CEO presenting strategy to your board**: pre-mortem before the board meeting, not after. Force your team to generate failure modes; bring them to the meeting; address them before being asked.
- **If you're a CFO reviewing strategic investments**: the management-systems question (Martin #5) is yours. Without enabling systems, capabilities atrophy. Watch for strategy that's all about new capabilities and silent on the systems to maintain them.
- **If you're a non-CEO exec reviewing the strategy of a peer division**: the Helmer 7 Powers question is the most efficient screen. "Which of these are we building?" surfaces clarity.

## How thinking has evolved

- **Pre-2024**: corpus has Helmer (7 Powers, [2023-09-21](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/business-strategy-with-hamilton-helmer)) and Rumelt (Good Strategy Bad Strategy, [2024-02-29](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/good-strategy-bad-strategy-richard-rumelt)) episodes.
- **2024-07-25**: Roger Martin — 5 essential questions framework. Becomes the default strategy-evaluation lens in the corpus.
- **2024-2025**: Annie Duke, Mihika Kapoor, Jag Duggal episodes layer additional decision and strategy lenses.
- **2026**: Evan Spiegel — "distribution is the most important moat" — closing the AI-era moat conversation.

## Inflection points

- **Helmer + Rumelt episodes (2023-2024)**: corpus formalizes the "what makes a strategy real?" diagnostic vocabulary.
- **Roger Martin 2024**: 5-questions becomes the default board-meeting frame.
- **AI-era Howie-Liu question (2025)**: "if we were founding AI-native today" — additional 2026 strategy diagnostic.
