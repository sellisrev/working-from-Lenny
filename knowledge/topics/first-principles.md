# First-principles thinking

## Current consensus

Lenny "First-principles thinking" (2024) plus Sam Schillace ("How to be more innovative", Microsoft deputy CTO, creator of Google Docs).

### What first-principles thinking is
- **Reasoning from foundational truths**, not from analogies or convention.
- **Rebuilding mental models from the ground up** rather than inheriting received wisdom.
- **Stripping away assumptions** to find what's actually constraining the problem.

### When it works
- **Genuinely novel problems** where the analogies don't fit.
- **Problems where convention is the bottleneck.** AI-era reframings often require this — every AI-product team is hitting "what would we do if we weren't bound by the way SaaS works?" moments.
- **High-stakes architectural decisions.** When the wrong choice locks you in for years.

### When it doesn't work
- **Most everyday problems.** Established patterns are usually correct; first-principles reasoning from scratch is wasteful overhead.
- **Without domain knowledge.** First-principles reasoning by someone without context produces overconfident bad answers.
- **As a substitute for talking to customers.** Cf. [continuous-discovery](continuous-discovery.md). Customers are the ground truth; reasoning alone often misses what they actually want.

### The Sam Schillace contribution (2024)
- **Innovation requires "messy middle" tolerance.** First-principles thinking produces messy intermediate states; impatient organizations kill novel ideas before they crystallize.
- **Most novel ideas look bad until they don't.** The framework's value is sustaining belief through the period when the idea looks wrong.

### Common failure modes
- **First-principles reasoning when convention is correct.** Most things have well-established correct answers.
- **Performative first-principles.** "Reasoning from first principles" as a flex when actually doing standard pattern-matching.
- **Skipping evidence.** Reasoning is necessary but not sufficient; ground in customer signal and external evidence.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-era product decisions often require first-principles reasoning** because the conventions don't yet exist or are decaying.
- **Howie Liu's "if we were founding AI-native today, what would we do?"** (cf. [ai-org-restructuring](ai-org-restructuring.md)) is a first-principles reframing applied to org design.
- **Cat Wu / Anthropic** ships features in research-preview mode, treating each as first-principles iteration rather than convention-following.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO contemplating a big strategic shift**: first-principles thinking is high-leverage. Don't skip it for decisions that lock in for years.
- **If you're an engineer or designer**: most engineering / design problems have established patterns. Apply first-principles selectively to novel architecture decisions.
- **If you're an exec hearing "we should reason from first principles"**: ask what foundational truths they're actually grounding in. If the answer is "I just feel this is right," it's not first-principles; it's intuition with a fancy label.
