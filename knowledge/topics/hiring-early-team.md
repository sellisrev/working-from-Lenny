# Hiring the early team (cross-functional)

## Current consensus

Early-stage hiring is a small set of patterns that have held since 2021 with one significant 2024-2026 update around AI tooling. The durable principles:

1. **Network is the highest-leverage early channel.** Most strong early hires (1st-5th) come from the founder's prior companies, investors, or co-founder's network. Public/inbound channels become useful around hire 10+. (Siqi Chen, Runway; Keith Rabois citing PayPal)
2. **Recruiting is pitching, not sales.** Treat candidate pitches like investor pitches — custom decks, scenario maps of outcomes, full transparency about risk. Several founders ([Reed McGinley-Stempel at Stytch](https://stytch.com/), [Brandon Hill at Vori](https://vori.com/)) explicitly equate the closing process to a fundraise.
3. **Best talent wants to work with best talent.** Quality compounds and becomes self-recruiting after a critical mass; lowering the bar to fill an immediate need produces durable damage. (Nick Handel, Transform; Siqi Chen, Runway; Julianna Lamb, Stytch)
4. **Define values first.** Without articulated values you cannot hire for them, and you'll attract ambiguous fit. (Nick Handel)
5. **Reference checks scale beyond what most teams do.** Tony Xu at DoorDash does 20 references per senior hire. Frame the question correctly: "Is X capable of being a world-class operator?" gets very different signal than "Did X do well in their last role?"
6. **30-day "would I hire again?" feedback loop.** Per Keith Rabois citing research, the 30-day judgment is approximately as accurate as the 1-2 year judgment. Use it; share results across the hiring team.
7. **Barrels vs ammunition.** Most companies have a small number of people who can drive inception-to-completion ("barrels") and many people who execute against direction ("ammunition"). PayPal had 12-17 barrels in 254 people. Most good companies have 2-3 barrels. Adding ammunition without barrels increases coordination tax without output. (Vinod Khosla via Keith Rabois)

The 2024-2026 update:

8. **AI-tooling fluency is now table stakes for builder roles.** "Have you shipped something you vibe-coded?" is now a more useful signal than "Have you written a PRD?" — both for PMs and adjacent functions. The "builder" archetype Singhal describes is the emergent dominant hire-for profile in 2026 ([ai-replacing-pms.md](ai-replacing-pms.md)). Conservative companies that don't update their hiring rubric for this miss the strongest candidates and over-index on legacy proxies.

**For non-PM consumers** of this knowledge:

- **If you're a founder or CEO hiring your early team**: do not skip steps 1-7 above just because you're hiring AI-savvy people. AI fluency is necessary but not sufficient; the legacy fundamentals about network, pitching, and references still dominate outcomes.
- **If you're an engineering or design leader on the hiring panel**: don't run "give me a code/design exercise" as your only signal. The cross-functional check (could this person partner with my function on judgment, not just deliverables?) matters more than ever as boundaries blur. Cf. [working-with-pms-as-engineer](working-with-pms-as-engineer.md), [working-with-pms-as-designer](working-with-pms-as-designer.md).
- **If you're a non-PM hiring manager hiring your first PM**: Lenny's primer ([when to hire your first PM](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/when-to-hire-your-first-product-manager)) and Singhal v2 framing apply. Hire a "builder" PM (someone who can ship a prototype, take ownership of a roadmap, and engage cross-functionally on judgment), not an "information mover." See [hiring-pms-as-non-pm](hiring-pms-as-non-pm.md) for the specific framing.
- **If you're an investor or board member**: ask portfolio CEOs how many barrels they have. The answer is usually 2-3 even at $50M+ ARR companies (Jack Altman at Loudouns gave 2 as the answer for a "pretty damn big company"). Stack-ranking burn against barrel count is a useful diligence frame.

## How thinking has evolved

Earliest takes (corpus, oldest first):

- **2021-07-06**: Lenny — synthesis of advice from a half-dozen early-stage founders. Five themes: network, pitch, raise the bar, closing experience, define values. ([Winning at early-stage hiring](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/winning-at-early-stage-hiring))

- **2022-03-22**: Lenny — interviewing PMs as a sub-genre of hiring; bias-elimination strategies become a recurring theme.

- **2022-05**: Brian Balfour and Lenny on "Six rules of hiring for growth" — emphasizes role specificity (sourcing-led vs ops-led growth roles) and explicit unit-economics expectations.

Middle period:

- **2023-10-03**: Lenny's seven-part B2B series part 6 — recommends founder-led sales until $300-500k ARR before first sales hire; "first hire should probably be a hungry senior AE." Aligned with PMF-first hiring philosophy.

- **2023-11-14**: When they hired their first PM — case studies suggest most successful companies hired their first PM later than expected (often after 50+ employees), reinforcing the "do it yourself first" pattern.

Recent takes (last six months):

- **2026-04-12**: Keith Rabois — the deepest single-episode treatment of barrel/ammunition framework, hire feedback loops, references, and skill-network sourcing. Worth reading (with [caveats around his other claims](../cautions/ai-replacing-pms.md)).

- **2026-04-19**: Nikhyl Singhal — bifurcation of PM hiring: companies will shed 30k and rehire 8k AI-first. Implication for hiring teams: redesign the rubric. The legacy "PM as roadmap-owner" hiring rubric is producing exactly the wrong shortlist.

External current consensus (web, weighted recent + AI-aware):

- a16z, First Round Review, and Reforge content from late 2025/early 2026 broadly aligns with the "AI-fluency is table stakes" framing. The deeper hiring fundamentals (network, references, pitching) have not shifted; they've just been applied to a different candidate profile.

## Inflection points

- **Late 2024 / early 2025: AI-tool fluency moves from differentiator to expected**: Cursor + Claude Code adoption normalizes. By 2026 a candidate without AI-tooling familiarity reads like a 2015 candidate without git familiarity to most AI-native hiring panels.
- **Lenny's 2026 PM job market report**: confirms PM roles at 3-year high. Hiring is up, not down. Recalibrates the assumption that early-stage hiring is a buyer's market for talent.
- **Builder PM emergence (2025-2026)**: "Builders Wanted" (Singhal) reshapes early-stage hiring rubrics; the "is this person a barrel?" question (Rabois) gains new operational meaning when the same person can prototype, ship, and own outcomes.

## Open questions

- How does the legacy "first PM hire" timing advice (post-PMF, often 50+ headcount) hold when AI tooling means you can ship product without a PM far longer — but also means a builder PM can substitute for several legacy roles? No corpus consensus.
- How does junior-builder development work when senior hires from the founder's network are the path of least resistance for the early team? The corpus has not addressed this since AI tooling changed the apprenticeship pipeline.
- For early teams of 10-20 with no in-house recruiter: does the 20-references-per-senior-hire bar (DoorDash) generalize, or is it specific to leaders with strong network reach?
