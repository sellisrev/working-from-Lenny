# IC PM to manager-of-managers transition

## Current consensus

Two distinct transitions are conflated under "becoming a manager"; corpus consensus:

### Transition 1: IC PM → PM Manager (manages PMs directly)
- **Different job, not "more of the same."** Camille Fournier's *The Manager's Path* canon: most surprising thing about becoming a manager is how different the work is.
- **Player-coach reality.** First-line PM managers usually still own some product work directly while managing 2-5 PMs. Pure management (no IC work) typically comes at director level.
- **Hardest skills**: giving feedback, hiring, prioritizing your time across PMs, delegating without losing context.
- **Fastest-failure mode**: missing the IC work too much. PMs who become managers and resent not coding/specing struggle.

### Transition 2: PM Manager → Manager of Managers
- **Even more different than the first transition.** Now you're a step removed from the work; you manage through your PM managers.
- **Cultural and strategic leadership intensifies.** You're now visible to the whole org.
- **You can't fix things by doing them.** Your levers are people, process, vision, hiring.

### When NOT to become a manager
- Many great ICs are bad managers. Lenny "Moving from IC product manager to manager of product managers" (2020): test the waters before committing.
- IC track at staff/principal level is increasingly viable. Some corpus guests (Shreyas Doshi, etc.) explicitly chose IC at senior+.
- Don't take a manager role for the title alone; the work is genuinely different.

### Camille Fournier's surprising points
- **Most managers do too many 1:1s.** 30 min weekly with N reports adds up; some can be biweekly or async-replaced.
- **Major rewrites are often a trap.** First-time managers tempted to rewrite the team's setup; usually wrong.
- **Heuristics for IC-to-manager timing**: 4-7 years as senior IC before management is the corpus norm; shorter often produces immature managers.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling shifts the IC ceiling upward.** Senior+ ICs can do more without delegation; the management premium shrinks for some kinds of work.
- **Smaller management spans at AI-native companies.** Cat Wu's Anthropic team has lighter management overhead because skills overlap.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineering manager**: the IC-to-manager transition pattern is the same. Camille Fournier's book is the cross-functional canonical reference.
- **If you're a designer considering management**: similar pattern; the "do design" addiction is the most common stumbling block.
- **If you're an exec deciding to promote an IC into management**: 4-7 years senior IC + demonstrated mentorship is the corpus-typical bar.
