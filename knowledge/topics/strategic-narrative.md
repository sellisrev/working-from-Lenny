# Strategic narrative for product

## Current consensus

Andy Raskin's "strategic narrative" framework is the corpus-canonical approach. ([The power of strategic narrative](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-power-of-strategic-narrative), 2023). Reinforced by Donna Lichaw, Nancy Duarte, and Stewart Butterfield's "We Don't Sell Saddles Here" essay.

### Andy Raskin's structural pattern
1. **Name the change in the world.** What's shifting that makes the old way of doing things obsolete?
2. **Name the winners and losers** of that change.
3. **Show the promised land** — what's possible for the winners.
4. **Position your product** as the path to the promised land.
5. **Bring evidence** that you can deliver.

This structure is canonical for: pitch decks, all-hands talks, sales narrative, fundraising stories, even product launches. The pattern is durable because it maps to how humans process change.

### Why strategic narrative beats feature lists
- **Features are commodity.** Anyone can build features.
- **The change-and-promised-land frame** taps into the audience's existing concerns about market shifts.
- **Naming winners and losers** forces the audience to identify with one side.
- **Evidence at the end** lands harder when the narrative has set up why it matters.

### Common failure modes
- **Generic change framing.** "AI is changing everything" doesn't differentiate.
- **No clear losers.** Without losers, "winners" feels promotional rather than directional.
- **Promised land too vague.** "We help you do X better" is not a promised land.
- **Skipping evidence.** The narrative needs to ground in customer stories or data; pure rhetoric isn't enough.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI is the dominant change-narrative.** Every B2B narrative now includes AI as the catalyst. Differentiation requires being more specific about which AI shift.
- **Distribution-as-moat (Spiegel)** is becoming the new "winner" framing in some narratives.
- **AEO + answer engines** reward sharp narrative — vague stories get summarized away; sharp ones get cited verbatim.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO or founder**: invest serious time in your strategic narrative. It shapes hiring, fundraising, sales, partnerships.
- **If you're a CMO**: strategic narrative drives content, messaging, brand.
- **If you're a sales lead**: use Raskin's structure for big pitches. Most B2B closes happen because the buyer was sold on the change-and-promised-land frame, not on features.

## How thinking has evolved

- **Pre-corpus**: Andy Raskin's blog and consulting work codified the framework over the 2010s.
- **2023-12**: Andy Raskin on Lenny's Podcast — corpus-canonical episode.
- **2024-2025**: Lenny "How to build a killer sales pitch" piece reinforces the same structure.
- **2026**: AI-era narratives dominate; sharper differentiation required as everyone uses similar frames.
