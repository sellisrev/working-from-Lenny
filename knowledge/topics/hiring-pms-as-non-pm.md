# Hiring PMs as a non-PM hiring manager

Written for founders, engineering leaders, CEOs, and other non-PM hiring managers tasked with hiring product managers without having the role context themselves.

## Current consensus

Six durable principles + three 2025-2026 updates.

### Durable principles (2022-2024 era, still hold)

1. **Structure the process; don't wing it.** Lenny's recommended structure (2022, still canonical): recruiter screen (30 min) → hiring manager screen (30 min) → 4-5 hour interview day in two parts (panel + project). Keep questions consistent across candidates with predefined good / bad answers. Iris Bohnet (HBR): structured interviews remove subjectivity.

2. **The project portion is the most informative.** Lenny: "I couldn't imagine a PM interview without it." Stewart Butterfield (Slack): "performance on the project was closely related to their success at Slack over the course of years." Use a real challenge from your business; brief the candidate; give them 1-3 days; review with the panel.

3. **Test the right skills, not all skills.** A short list works better than a comprehensive list. Lenny's recommended panel:
   - **Collaboration**: "Tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineer..."
   - **Execution**: "Walk me through a 3-9 month project end to end."
   - **Strategy**: "Pick a product you worked on; talk me through the strategy."
   - **Customer insights**: "Tell me about user research that had big product impact."
   - **Impact**: "What's the most important product you shipped? Would it have shipped without you, and why?"

4. **Use the single-question power tool.** Lenny's "[How to learn the most about a candidate from a single interview question](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-learn-the-most-about-a-candidate-from-a-single-interview-question)" (2024-02) compiles 25 high-signal questions from 150 podcast guests. For non-PM hiring managers, use 1-2 of these in the hiring-manager screen.

5. **Don't lower the bar to fill the role.** Network-based first hires + raised-bar discipline (cf. [hiring-early-team](hiring-early-team.md)). Hiring a mediocre PM compounds quality damage across the team.

6. **Make the closing experience pitch-quality.** Lenny + Brandon Hill (Vori): treat candidate-closing like investor pitching — custom decks, transparent risks, structured economic-outcomes thinking. Treats the candidate as an investor of their time.

### 2025-2026 updates (what's changed)

7. **Hire builders, not information movers.** Cf. [ai-replacing-pms](ai-replacing-pms.md). Nikhyl Singhal (April 2026): the "information mover" PM archetype is being shed; the "builder" PM is being hired aggressively. As a non-PM hiring manager, screen for: have they shipped something themselves? Are they comfortable in Cursor / Claude Code / Bolt? Are they hands-on with prototypes? Cat Wu (Anthropic): "engineers with great product taste" is a viable PM substitute at AI-native companies.

8. **AI-fluency is now a screening signal.** "Tell me about an AI feature you shipped" is now a useful screen question. A candidate who hasn't shipped any AI feature in 2026 is not necessarily wrong but should be probed for why; "the org didn't let me" is acceptable, "I didn't think to" is not.

9. **The 30-day rehire test (Keith Rabois) is more important than ever.** "30 days after the hire, would you make the same decision?" Per Rabois citing research: 30-day judgment is approximately as accurate as 1-2 year judgment. Capture this signal; share across the hiring team. AI-era candidates are easier to over-impress in interview (polished prototypes, articulate framing); the 30-day signal cuts through.

## What this means for non-PM hiring managers specifically

- **If you're an eng leader or CTO hiring your first PM**: the question is not "do I need a PM?" — it's "what kind of builder do I need who can do PM-shaped work?" Cat Wu's Anthropic example: many engineers ship end-to-end without dedicated PMs. Before you hire a PM, consider whether an engineer with product taste is the better hire. If you do hire a PM, make it a builder, not a coordinator.
- **If you're a founder doing your first PM hire**: do not delegate the screening process to a recruiter who hasn't been trained on the PM-builder distinction. Recruiters trained 2018-2024 will surface "information mover" archetypes. Be in the loop on the criteria.
- **If you're a non-tech CEO at an enterprise company**: traditional career PMs may still be the right hire for your context (regulated industries, very large teams, deeply technical domain expertise). The "builder PM" framing is most applicable to AI-native or rapidly-scaling product orgs. Diagnose your context, then hire.
- **If you're a sales / GTM leader**: when interviewing PMs as a non-hiring panelist, focus on: do they engage with sales context? Do they understand willingness-to-pay? Have they ever closed a deal or shadowed customer support? PMs without sales empathy will create roadmap-vs-pipeline conflicts you'll have to manage.
- **If you're a designer or design lead**: when on a PM panel, evaluate: do they think in terms of user experience, not just user "value"? Have they shipped something where they personally chose between two design options? Cf. [working-with-pms-as-designer](working-with-pms-as-designer.md).

## Open questions

- For very early-stage startups (pre-PMF) hiring their first PM: should you hire at all? Lenny's [When to hire your first PM](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/when-to-hire-your-first-product-manager) leans toward "later than you think." AI tooling extends "later" further.
- Do AI-era PM interviews need fundamentally new question types, or are the 2022-2024 questions sufficient with surface adjustments? The corpus has not yet codified AI-specific PM interview frameworks.
- For domain-specialized PM roles (regulatory PM, enterprise PM, growth PM): how do you weight builder vs domain expertise? Open question.
