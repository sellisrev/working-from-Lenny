# How AI is changing or replacing the PM role

## Current consensus

The PM role is bifurcating, not disappearing. As of April 2026, two facts hold simultaneously:

1. **More open PM roles globally than in 3+ years** ([Lenny's 2026 job market report](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/state-of-the-product-job-market-in-2025); confirmed by Nikhyl Singhal [Why half of product managers are in trouble, 2026-04-19](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-half-of-product-managers-are-in-trouble)).
2. **Massive shedding ahead followed by massive rehiring** — Singhal predicts a 12-24 month period where companies shed 30k and hire 8k, where the 8k are "AI first."

Two PM archetypes diverge sharply:

- **The "information mover" PM** (translates between functions, owns roadmaps, runs status meetings, writes PRDs as their primary deliverable) — Singhal: "essentially going to become a dinosaur." This was already the failing archetype before AI; AI accelerates its retirement.
- **The "builder" PM** (hands-on, opinionated, ships, can prototype with Cursor / Claude Code, makes calls on what to build) — Singhal: "compensation is at an all-time high. They have more offers than they've ever seen. They see their next job maybe being a founder, maybe being a CEO." 14 of 125 in his head-of-product community founded a company in the last 12 months (vs. 1 of 60-70 three years prior).

**For non-PM consumers** of this knowledge:

- **If you're a hiring manager**: do not hire a PM whose pitch is "I orchestrate alignment." Hire PMs who can show you something they shipped (vibe-coded prototypes count). Cf. [hiring-pms-as-non-pm](hiring-pms-as-non-pm.md).
- **If you're an engineering leader**: expect PMs to ship code. Cat Wu (Anthropic, Head of Product, Claude Code) frames the PM job as "as code becomes much cheaper to write, the thing that becomes more valuable is deciding what to write."
- **If you're a CEO/founder**: don't disband the PM function based on "AI replaces PMs" rhetoric. The role is *changing* (toward judgment + cross-functional orchestration of agents), not disappearing. Lenny's job market data refutes the wholesale-replacement claim.
- **If you're a designer or engineer**: the "PM" boundary is blurring. Singhal: "lots of engineers are builders, lots of designers are builders, lots of marketing folks are builders." You may already be doing PM-shaped work; learn to claim and own it.

What stays valuable: judgment ("evaluating whether the thing we're changing is good or bad"), product taste, customer empathy, narrative skill, conviction. What stays under threat: writing PRDs as a primary value-add, orchestrating standups, owning the Jira tracker, being the "translator" between functions.

## How thinking has evolved

Earliest takes (corpus, oldest first):

- **2024-04-09**: Lenny — surveyed his audience on which PM skills AI would replace; result: audience expected hard skills (coding/design/writing) to survive and soft skills (leadership/influence/strategy) to be replaced. Lenny argued the opposite: soft skills will become MORE important. ([How AI will impact product management](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-ai-will-impact-product-management))

- **2025-02-09**: Karina Nguyen (OpenAI Research, ex-Anthropic) — confirmed Lenny's framing: "soft skills are going to be more and more important... AI is actually really good at [hard skills] because it's taking a bunch of data, synthesizing it." ([Lenny's Podcast](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-soft-skills-are-the-future-of-work))

Middle period:

- **2025-12-17 retrospective**: Lenny's Best of 2024 highlighted "Why PMs are best positioned to thrive in an AI world" as a top piece — framing was optimistic.

Recent takes (last six months):

- **2026-04-12**: Keith Rabois (Khosla Ventures) — went much further than Lenny's earlier framing: "the idea of a PM makes no sense in the future. The skill is more like being a CEO now, which is what are we building and why?" ([Hard truths about building in the AI era](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xCd9ykretlg)). **This claim attracted significant audience pushback (see [`../cautions/ai-replacing-pms.md`](../cautions/ai-replacing-pms.md)).**

- **2026-04-19**: Nikhyl Singhal (returning guest, 3 years after first appearance) — calibrated the bifurcation with data: massive shedding + massive rehiring; PM job market at 3-year high; 14 of 125 head-of-product community members became founders in the last year. "We are seeing a complete renaissance for the product industry, but it comes with a lot of strings attached." ([Lenny's Podcast](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-half-of-product-managers-are-in-trouble))

External current consensus (web, weighted recent + AI-aware):

- HBR / industry coverage from late-2025/early-2026 broadly aligns with the bifurcation thesis. The "PM is dead" extreme view (Rabois) is not the consensus.
- Anthropic's Cat Wu (April 2026): Anthropic continues to hire PMs; the role at Anthropic is to "set clear goals, create repeatable shipping process, and manage cross-functional partners' expectations" — not orchestrate engineering tickets.

## Inflection points

- **ChatGPT public release (2022-11)**: started the wave; the PM question was abstract until tools arrived.
- **Lenny's April 2024 newsletter**: first widespread framing in the corpus that "soft skills will dominate" and PMs aren't going away if they adapt. Set the optimistic baseline.
- **Cursor at $300M ARR (April 2025)**: proof-of-concept that AI tooling could reach enterprise scale. After this, "PMs need to use AI tools" stopped being a contrarian take.
- **Lenny's 2026 PM job market report**: empirical refutation of wholesale "PMs are being replaced" narrative — open roles at 3-year high. Reframed the conversation as "which PM archetype" rather than "if PM."
- **Keith Rabois "PM makes no sense" claim + audience pushback (April 2026)**: a visible community moment where the extreme view got called out, including the 191-vote top comment "fascinating conversation, from the people who will never be affected by any of it" and the 47-vote factual rebuttal that Rabois's DoorDash example contradicted DoorDash's own founding story.
- **Nikhyl Singhal v1 -> v2 (June 2023 -> April 2026)**: Same guest, 3-year gap. v1 advice was traditional PM career-pathing. v2 is bifurcation framing. **High-confidence inflection** because the same speaker explicitly updated their stance with framing change ("information mover -> dinosaur").

## Open questions

- Will the PM job market still be at a 3-year high after the predicted "shedding" wave Nikhyl Singhal forecasts? Lenny's data is current; 12-24 months will tell.
- Does the "builder PM" archetype generalize beyond AI-native companies? At a non-tech enterprise (regulated industry, very large team), the "information mover" function may persist longer.
- Is the "PM as CEO of the product" framing (Rabois, also classic PM canon) operationally meaningful when CEO authority is structurally absent? The 50-vote comment ("PMs that aren't asking what are we building and why are product owners, not product managers") suggests the practitioner community has long disliked the "information mover" archetype but did not need AI to dislike it.
- Does junior PM development still work when the entry-level "translator" tasks are automated? No corpus consensus on the apprenticeship pipeline.

## Cross-corpus reinforcement (How I AI as secondary signal)

How I AI episodes (2026-04 onward) consistently feature non-PM functions (engineers, customer support, designers) doing PM-shaped work via AI tools (Brian Scanlan, Al Chen, etc.). This **reinforces** the main-corpus claim that the PM boundary is blurring across functions — the "builders are everywhere" thesis Singhal raises has corroborating evidence in the secondary corpus.
