# Storytelling and narrative for leaders

## Current consensus

Storytelling is a load-bearing leadership skill, not a marketing-only one. Corpus consensus, anchored in:
- **Nancy Duarte** (canonical Lenny episode on presentations and narrative).
- **Matthew Dicks** (*Storyworthy*, 2024 episode).
- **Andy Raskin** (strategic narrative, 2023).
- **Donna Lichaw** (*The Leader's Journey*).

### The core principles
1. **Story = problem + change + meaning.** Without a problem there's no tension; without change there's no narrative; without meaning the audience doesn't care.
2. **Specific beats abstract.** "Sarah, a hospitality HR manager at a 200-person hotel..." beats "Our target customer."
3. **Show the stakes.** What happens if the protagonist (you, your customer, your team) fails?
4. **Lead with the hook.** First 30 seconds determine whether the audience invests.
5. **Edit ruthlessly.** Stories that work are ones that have been told 50 times and refined each time.

### Andy Raskin's strategic narrative pattern (canonical pitch structure)
1. **Name the change** in the world.
2. **Identify the winners and losers** in that change.
3. **Show the promised land** for those who adapt.
4. **Position your product** as the path to the promised land.
5. **Bring evidence** that you can deliver.

### Matthew Dicks's homework-for-life practice
- **Daily 5-minute reflection**: what was the most story-worthy thing today? Builds the story-spotting skill.
- **Stories aren't about events; they're about change.** A great story has a moment where the protagonist becomes different.

### Donna Lichaw's leader's journey
- **Three acts** (Joseph Campbell-style): call to action, transformation, return changed.
- **Apply to your own leader narrative**: what's your story? Why do you do this work? Articulate it; refine it; deploy it.

### Common failure modes
- **Too much data, not enough story.** Numbers anchor stories; they don't substitute for them.
- **Generic storytelling.** Customer-archetype stories with no specifics. Bring real names, real numbers, real conversations.
- **Pretending to be an inspiring narrator.** Some leaders aren't natural storytellers; that's fine. Plain-spoken honesty often wins. Don't force it.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-assisted writing speeds up story drafting** but produces generic output if used carelessly. Use AI for first-draft and then heavily revise.
- **Live demos and prototypes substitute for verbal storytelling** in some product pitches. Cf. [ai-prototyping](ai-prototyping.md).

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a designer**: storytelling is core craft. Don't outsource it to PM/marketing.
- **If you're an engineer pitching internal projects**: a working prototype + 60 seconds of story beats a 30-slide deck.
- **If you're a CEO**: your company's narrative is one of your highest-leverage outputs. Worth investing serious time in refining.
- **If you're a sales lead**: every great pitch is a story. Andy Raskin's framework is the bestseller for B2B sales.
