# Spotting top 1% startups early

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "How to spot a top 1% startup early" (2024) + Mike Maples Jr. *Pattern Breakers* + Dalton Caldwell (YC) + Brian Halligan ("never been easier to start; never been harder to scale", 2026).

### What the corpus says about top 1% startups
1. **Strong founders, not just smart founders.** Resilience + judgment + relentlessness.
2. **Inflection-driven, insight-led.** Cf. [pattern-breakers](pattern-breakers.md). Riding a real market shift with a non-obvious insight.
3. **Customer obsession that's behavioral, not stated.** Founder talks to customers daily, not weekly.
4. **Velocity from day one.** Top startups ship fast at every stage.
5. **Recruiting magnetism.** They attract better talent than their stage suggests they should.
6. **Honest with themselves.** Top founders update beliefs based on evidence; less-strong founders defend bad ideas.

### What's NOT a reliable signal
- **Pedigree.** Stanford / MIT / ex-FAANG founders fail at similar rates to less-pedigreed ones.
- **Initial vision quality.** Many top startups pivoted (cf. [pivots-art](pivots-art.md)). Vision update matters more than vision quality at start.
- **Press / hype.** Top 1% often quiet at seed; loud-at-seed often dies.
- **Burn rate.** High burn doesn't predict success; efficient burn signals discipline.

### What investors look for
- **Exceptional team.** First filter; the rest follows.
- **Demonstrated founder-market fit.** Founders who've spent years in the problem domain.
- **Customer evidence.** Even at pre-seed, evidence of pull.
- **Clarity about the inflection.** Founders who can name why now is the right time.

### Common failure modes
- **Over-indexing on charisma.** Charismatic founders sometimes deliver; sometimes don't.
- **Single-meeting decisions.** Multiple touchpoints reveal more.
- **Pattern-matching to past winners.** The next top startup looks different from the last one.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-native startups** show different signals (faster scale, smaller teams, higher per-employee leverage).
- **Mike Maples + Dalton Caldwell + Andrew Wilkinson** all 2024-2025 corpus references on idea / founder evaluation.
- **Brian Halligan (2026)**: starting is easier; scaling is harder. The bottleneck has shifted.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an angel investor**: invest in teams, not ideas. Ideas pivot; teams either persist or don't.
- **If you're an existing employee considering joining a startup**: same evaluation criteria as investors.
- **If you're a CEO mentoring founders**: spot the top 1% by observing how they update beliefs over weeks, not how they pitch on day 1.
