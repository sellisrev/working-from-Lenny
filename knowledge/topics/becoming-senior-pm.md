# Becoming a senior PM

## Current consensus

The transition from PM to senior PM (and then to staff / principal / lead) is mostly about scope and judgment, not effort. The corpus consensus:

1. **Scope expands; specifics narrow.** A junior PM owns features; a senior PM owns outcomes; a staff PM owns areas; a principal PM owns directions. At each step, what you personally do shrinks; what you're responsible for grows.
2. **Judgment > execution.** Junior PMs are graded on shipping; senior PMs are graded on choosing what to ship. The skill is choice quality, not throughput.
3. **Cross-functional leadership without authority becomes mandatory.** Cf. [pm-influence](pm-influence.md). Senior PMs influence engineers, designers, sales, and execs simultaneously.
4. **Strategy fluency.** Senior PMs operate up the strategy stack (Mission → Vision → Strategy → Goals → Roadmap → Task). Junior PMs work in the bottom layers; senior PMs touch all of them.
5. **Defending big bets.** Cf. [defending-big-bets](defending-big-bets.md). Senior PMs run multi-quarter strategic initiatives that ship under cover of incremental wins. Junior PMs don't.

### What changed in 2025-2026

- **Builders are now senior** in a way that wasn't true in 2022. Singhal: builders are wanted at every level. The senior PM who is *not* hands-on has a tougher career path than the senior PM who is.
- **AI fluency is now table stakes for senior+**. A senior PM in 2026 who hasn't shipped an AI feature is increasingly unhirable for AI-product roles.
- **Founder-track is more common.** Singhal: 14 of 125 in his head-of-product community founded companies in the last year. The senior PM career path increasingly includes "go found something" as a viable next step, not just "become a director."

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a manager hiring a senior PM**: test scope of judgment, not throughput. Ask: "Tell me about the biggest call you made in the last year." Acceptable: a hard call with stakes. Bad: a feature shipped on time.
- **If you're a non-PM exec considering a PM transition (cf. Singhal builders-everywhere framing)**: senior PMs hire from product-adjacent backgrounds (engineering, design, ops). Build a portfolio of shipped product decisions, not just shipped artifacts.
- **If you're a board member at a growth-stage company**: the head of product's senior-PM bench depth is your single best succession-risk metric. Strong senior PMs leave when they can; thin bench is durable risk.
