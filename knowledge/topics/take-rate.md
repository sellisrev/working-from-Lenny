# Choosing a marketplace take rate

## Current consensus

Lenny "Choosing a take rate" (2021) is the corpus-canonical reference. Reinforced by Sarah Tavel, Ramesh Johari (Stanford), Benjamin Lauzier (Lyft, Thumbtack).

### What "take rate" is
- **The percentage of each transaction the marketplace keeps.** Uber's commission, Etsy's listing fee + transaction percentage, App Store's 30%.

### Benchmarks (Lenny canonical)
- **B2C transaction marketplaces**: typically 10-25%.
- **B2B marketplaces**: typically 5-15%.
- **Platform marketplaces** (App Store, Steam): historically 30%; under regulatory pressure.
- **Labor marketplaces**: 10-20%.
- **Consumer goods marketplaces**: 5-15%.

### How to think about take rate
1. **What's the value-add to suppliers?** High value-add (real demand acquisition) supports higher take. Low value-add (just listing) supports lower take.
2. **What's the supplier alternatives?** Marketplaces compete with direct sales channels; high-value suppliers can defect.
3. **What's the marketplace's lifecycle stage?** Lower take early to attract supply; raise as network effects compound.
4. **What's the pricing of competing marketplaces?** Don't be 5x more than alternatives.

### Take rate as growth lever
- **Lower take rate** attracts supply faster; lower margin per transaction.
- **Higher take rate** funds more growth investment; risks supply churn.
- **Tiered take rates** (high-volume sellers pay less) reward density.
- **Take rate increases are politically expensive.** Apple's 30% has driven decade-long regulatory scrutiny.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **Regulatory pressure on platform take rates.** Apple, Google App Store at 30% increasingly contested. EU DMA + multiple antitrust cases shaping the floor.
- **AI-mediated marketplaces** can offer higher value-add (Mercor's expert eval matching), supporting higher take rates.
- **Outcome-based take rates** rising for autonomous-agent marketplaces (Devin per-PR, Mercor per-validated-eval).

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a marketplace operator**: take rate setting is one of 2-3 highest-leverage decisions. Don't change frequently; getting it right early is durable advantage.
- **If you're an investor evaluating a marketplace**: ask how take rate compares to alternatives, what value-add justifies it, and what the regulatory exposure is.
- **If you're a supplier on a marketplace**: take rate disputes only matter if the marketplace's value-add is in question. If supply has alternatives, take rate becomes negotiable.
