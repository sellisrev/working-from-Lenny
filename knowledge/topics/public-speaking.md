# Public speaking and persuasive communication

## Current consensus

Anchored in Matt Abrahams (Stanford GSB, "How to speak more confidently and persuasively", 2024) + Tristan de Montebello (Ultraspeaking, "Why most public speaking advice is wrong", 2024) + Wes Kao communication content.

### Matt Abrahams's framing
- **Speaking is performance + structure.** Both matter; structure is more learnable.
- **AAA pattern**: Anticipate questions, Acknowledge what's hard, Answer concretely.
- **Reduce filler with structure, not willpower.** A clear 3-point structure stops the "um" before it starts.
- **Practice in low-stakes settings.** Toastmasters, podcasts, internal presentations — accumulating reps beats reading books.

### Tristan de Montebello's contrarian take (Ultraspeaking)
- **Most public-speaking advice is wrong.** "Imagine the audience naked" / "memorize your speech" / "speak slowly" — generic advice without context.
- **Anxiety is information, not weakness.** Speakers who fight anxiety often choke; speakers who use it perform.
- **Improvisation > memorization for most contexts.** Genuine engagement beats polished script.

### Wes Kao communication craft
- **Lead with the headline.** First sentence is the takeaway.
- **Specificity wins.** Numbers, dates, named decisions.
- **Pre-write hard talks.** Draft, revise, practice — not memorize.

### What works across contexts
- **Practice with feedback.** Recording yourself + watching produces faster improvement than just speaking more.
- **Calibrate to audience.** Same content needs different framing for engineers vs. execs vs. customers.
- **Storytelling over bullet points.** Cf. [storytelling](storytelling.md). Specific stories beat abstract claims.

### Common failure modes
- **Memorizing.** Memorized speeches sound memorized; stumbles destroy momentum.
- **Reading slides.** Audience can read; your job is to add what slides can't.
- **Apologizing for nerves.** Calls attention to nerves; usually makes things worse.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tools for talk prep** (drafting, structure, rehearsal feedback) reduce overhead.
- **Podcast guesting as primary public speaking** is increasingly common for founders / execs.
- **Direct social channels** mean leadership communication is now public-by-default.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO**: invest in speaking training. Cf. Lenny's recurring Ultraspeaking endorsements.
- **If you're a senior IC**: speaking skill caps career upside if poor. Worth investing in earlier than you think.
- **If you're a designer or engineer**: presenting work clearly is design work; treat it as such.
