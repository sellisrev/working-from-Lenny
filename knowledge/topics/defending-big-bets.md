# Defending your big bets

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "Defending your big bets" (2021) — the canonical corpus reference. Reinforced by Anneka Gupta on harnessing founder mode, Mihika Kapoor on conviction, and Annie Duke on kill criteria.

### The core operating principle: 80/20 cover fire
- **80% of the team's time** on short-term low-risk incremental wins.
- **20% on the high-risk, long-term big bet.**
- The incremental wins generate trust, hit KPIs, and provide cover for the bet to mature without being cut.
- Without cover fire, big bets are easy targets when priorities shift or fires emerge.

### Why this works (Lenny 2021)
- Leaders give the benefit of the doubt to teams who consistently ship.
- Resources are scarce; teams not showing impact lose them first.
- "We've been working on this for 6 months and have nothing to show" is the kill signal.
- "Here are 5 wins this quarter, plus we're 30% done on the big bet" is the survival signal.

### Tactical defenses
- **Communicate progress in small chunks.** Don't disappear for a quarter; produce visible artifacts monthly.
- **Stack-rank the big bet against alternatives.** Make leadership acknowledge what they'd be giving up to kill it.
- **Use kill criteria to your advantage.** Cf. Annie Duke. Pre-define what would cause you to stop; if those conditions don't hit, you have a defensible case to continue.
- **Bring in cover.** Senior allies who'll vouch for the bet matter when leadership wavers.
- **Document customer signal.** Even early. "3 customers told us they'd pay $X for this" is hard to dismiss.

### Common failure modes
- **All-in on the big bet.** No cover fire = easy target.
- **Pure-secret skunkworks.** Hidden bets get discovered eventually; surprise rarely helps.
- **No customer evidence.** Conviction without signal is fragile.
- **Inability to articulate "what does success look like?"** Vague success is unfundable.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling makes cover fire cheaper.** Small wins ship faster; the 80% slice is more productive. Some teams might now be 70/30 or 60/40 because AI cuts cover-fire cost.
- **Founder-mode IC CEOs** (cf. [founder-mode](founder-mode.md), [ai-org-restructuring](ai-org-restructuring.md)) often defend big bets by personally working on them; the cover-fire framing changes when the bet is led by leadership directly.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO sponsoring a big bet**: you are the cover. Your continued public commitment is what protects the team.
- **If you're an exec reviewing a big-bet pitch**: ask for the cover-fire plan AND the kill criteria. Both signal mature thinking.
- **If you're an engineer / designer on a big-bet team**: mid-quarter visibility is not bureaucracy; it's survival.
- **If you're a board member**: portfolio-level big-bet defense is your job. Founders need political cover from the board to hold the line.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2021-03**: Lenny "Defending your big bets" — foundational 80/20 framing.
- **2024**: Annie Duke on kill criteria; Mihika Kapoor on conviction.
- **2025-2026**: AI-tooling shifts cover-fire economics; founder-mode IC CEOs reshape the defense pattern.
