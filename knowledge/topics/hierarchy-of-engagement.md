# Hierarchy of engagement (Sarah Tavel)

## Current consensus

Sarah Tavel's hierarchy of engagement is the corpus-canonical model for thinking about user depth-of-engagement progression, originally from her time as Pinterest PM and now as a Benchmark partner.

### The hierarchy (from least to most engaged)
1. **Try.** First-time use; users explore.
2. **Habit.** Returns regularly; product becomes part of routine.
3. **Core action.** Engaged in the central value-creating loop (e.g., pinning, sharing, posting).
4. **Power user.** Beyond the core action; advanced features; investment in the product.
5. **Network locked.** User's network depends on continued use; switching cost compounds.

### Why hierarchy matters
- **Different users at different levels need different experiences.** A power-user feature that confuses tryers reduces overall engagement.
- **Optimize the level above where most users are.** If most users are at "habit," the lever is moving them to "core action." Going for "power user" features when most users haven't habituated is premature optimization.
- **Network-locked is the durable moat.** When users' networks depend on the product, churn craters.

### How this maps to other corpus frameworks
- **Cohort retention** (cf. [cohort-retention](cohort-retention.md)) is the measurement; hierarchy of engagement is the model.
- **Activation metric** (cf. [activation-metric](activation-metric.md)) maps roughly to "habit" or "core action" depending on the product.
- **Network effects** (Helmer 7 Powers) is the consequence of "network locked" users.

### Common failure modes
- **Single engagement metric (e.g., MAU).** Hides the distribution; treats trier and power user the same.
- **Optimizing for wrong level.** Pinterest's early growth came from optimizing the "try → habit" transition, not from power-user features.
- **Power-user fixation.** Some PMs over-index on the most engaged users; ignore this is a small slice for most products.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI products show non-traditional progression curves.** Many users hit "core action" within a single session (vibe-coding tools), but hit churn cliffs at capability limits before reaching "power user."
- **The framework is durable.** Sarah Tavel's hierarchy still applies; AI-product specifics need calibration.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a designer**: design for the level where most users actually are. Power-user features should be discoverable but not the default.
- **If you're a CMO or growth lead**: marketing copy should match the hierarchy level you're trying to move users to.
- **If you're an exec reviewing engagement metrics**: ask for distribution across hierarchy levels, not just aggregate engagement.

## How thinking has evolved

- **Pre-corpus**: Sarah Tavel's blog post (2014-ish) introduced the hierarchy framework.
- **2024**: Sarah Tavel on Lenny's Podcast — corpus reinforcement.
- **2025-2026**: AI-product engagement shapes diverge but hierarchy structure remains useful.
