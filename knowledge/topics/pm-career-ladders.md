# PM career ladders

## Current consensus

The corpus standardizes on a 5-level PM ladder, with parallel manager track:

### IC track
- **APM / Junior PM**: 0-2 years; ships features under tight supervision; learns the craft.
- **PM**: 2-5 years; owns features end-to-end; cross-functional leadership.
- **Senior PM**: 4-8 years; owns outcomes; multiple complex initiatives.
- **Staff PM**: 7+ years; owns areas; shapes strategy at the area level.
- **Principal PM**: 10+ years; owns directions; influences company strategy.

### Manager track
- **PM Manager / Group PM**: manages 2-5 PMs; player-coach role.
- **Director of Product**: manages PM Managers; owns multiple product areas.
- **VP / SVP Product**: company-level product leadership.
- **Chief Product Officer**: executive-level product strategy ownership.

### Cross-cutting principles
- **Scope expands; specifics narrow.** Each level owns wider work but does less personal execution.
- **Influence-without-authority requirement intensifies.** Staff/Principal PMs influence VPs without managing anyone.
- **The IC track is increasingly viable in tech.** "I never want to manage" is now a legitimate career direction at most companies.

### Variability across companies
- **Big-tech ladders** (Meta, Google, Amazon) are highly formalized; promotion criteria documented.
- **Startup ladders** are usually informal; titles inflate easily; calibration with bigtech is unreliable.
- **AI-native companies** (Anthropic, etc.) tend to flatten the ladder; titles matter less than shipped work.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **The boundary between IC PM and engineer has dissolved at AI-native companies.** Cat Wu's framing: hire for product taste regardless of background. The "PM ladder" is increasingly a "builder ladder" with PM as one path through it.
- **Founder-track legitimacy.** Some senior+ PMs skip the ladder entirely.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineering manager evaluating PM promotion candidates**: scope of judgment, not throughput, is the right signal at senior+.
- **If you're an HR / people leader designing your PM ladder**: don't copy big-tech ladders if you're a startup; calibrate to your context.
- **If you're a non-PM evaluating moving into PM**: lateral hires from engineering / design / ops typically enter at PM or Senior PM, not APM. Your years of professional experience translate.
