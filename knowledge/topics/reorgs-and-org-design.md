# Surviving reorgs and choosing org design

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "Surviving reorgs, tactically and emotionally" (2020) + Lenny "General management, functional, and hybrid models: Which org design works best for top companies?" (2024) + Heidi Helfand (*Dynamic Reteaming*).

### Org design archetypes
- **Functional** (engineering, design, product as separate orgs): clear specialization; weak cross-functional speed.
- **General management / business unit** (each BU has all functions): strong end-to-end ownership; coordination tax across BUs.
- **Hybrid** (functional shared services + BU-aligned product teams): most large public-tech companies (Airbnb, Stripe at scale, Microsoft).
- **Empowered teams** (Cagan model): cross-functional units, durable membership, problem-led not output-led.
- **Fast-thinking + slow-thinking** (Howie Liu / Airtable, 2025): explicit dual structure for AI-era cycle differences. Cf. [ai-org-restructuring](ai-org-restructuring.md).

### When to reorg
- **Strategy changes substantively.** Old org structure was for old strategy; new strategy needs new structure.
- **Coordination is failing.** Specific cross-team dysfunction is symptomatic of structural mismatch.
- **Scale crosses thresholds** (50, 150, 500, 1000 employees): each transition usually requires structural change.
- **AI-tooling adoption** in 2025-2026: many orgs need restructuring to enable AI-native work patterns.

### When NOT to reorg
- **As a substitute for hiring.** Restructuring won't fix bad people decisions.
- **As a substitute for strategic clarity.** If strategy is unclear, restructuring solves nothing.
- **Too frequently.** Each reorg costs ~6 months of throughput; consecutive reorgs = perpetual disruption.

### Surviving reorgs (Lenny 2020 IC perspective)
- **Don't panic.** Most reorgs feel worse than they are.
- **Lobby for the assignment you want.** Reorgs are when org structure is mutable; advocate.
- **Develop "skip-level" relationships before reorgs.** Networks survive structural change.
- **Document your value.** Performance evidence that survives the reorg helps placement.

### Heidi Helfand's *Dynamic Reteaming*
- **Teams change all the time; structure should normalize this.**
- **Five reteaming patterns**: one-by-one, grow-and-split, isolation, switching, merging.
- **Predictable reteaming reduces anxiety** vs. surprise reorgs.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-driven restructuring is its own reorg category.** Cf. [ai-org-restructuring](ai-org-restructuring.md). Howie Liu's Airtable + Asha Sharma "death of org charts" + Singhal's "shed 30k, hire 8k AI-first" forecast.
- **The reorg pattern is more frequent at AI-native companies.** Restructuring around new model capabilities, agent integration, product-velocity changes.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO**: don't reorg as cover for indecision. Reorg when strategy demands it; otherwise commit to the structure.
- **If you're a senior leader navigating a reorg**: lobbying behavior is appropriate; victim behavior is not.
- **If you're an IC**: most reorgs are not personal; some affect you. Distinguish.
- **If you're an HR / People leader**: Heidi Helfand's patterns + clear communication + predictability reduce reorg pain.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2020**: Lenny "Surviving reorgs" — IC perspective.
- **2023**: Heidi Helfand on dynamic reteaming.
- **2024-2026**: Org-design framings (functional vs hybrid vs general management); AI-driven restructuring becomes its own category.
