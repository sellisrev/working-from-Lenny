# Taking time off and sabbaticals

## Current consensus

The corpus has shifted significantly on time-off in the AI era. Anchored in Lenny "On taking time off" (2021) + "Three myths about sabbatical programs" (2021) + repeated 2024-2026 "taking the week off" newsletters as practice.

### Durable principles
1. **Time off is not a productivity loss; it's a productivity input.** People come back with better ideas, fresher energy, calibrated priorities.
2. **Sabbatical programs work** when set up correctly. Three common myths (Lenny 2021): they're too expensive (false: usually pay for themselves in retention), they're disruptive (false: forcing knowledge transfer is healthy), they're a perk for senior people only (false: junior people benefit most from learning ramp).
3. **Disconnection matters more than duration.** A fully-disconnected week often beats a partially-connected month.
4. **Boundary-setting is the hardest part.** Most people fail to take real time off because they keep peeking. Use auto-responder + remove app + tell people you're truly out.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI inflection: time off makes you better at AI.** Multiple corpus guests (Brian Scanlan / Intercom, Hilary Gridley) report colleagues coming back from time off having spent it exploring AI tools — and returning dramatically more capable. This argues for *more* PTO, not less, in 2026.
- **The "no days off" framing is increasingly flagged as out-of-touch.** Cf. [`../cautions/ai-replacing-pms.md`](../cautions/ai-replacing-pms.md). Audience pushback against this framing has crystallized.
- **Lenny himself models taking weeks off** (multiple "Taking the week off" newsletter entries). Practice over preaching.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO/founder**: model taking time off. Your reports won't until you do.
- **If you're an HR / People leader**: sabbatical programs (3-6 months after 5-7 years) are a high-leverage retention tool. The literature on outcomes is positive.
- **If you're an individual avoiding time off**: usually not because the work needs you; usually because you're avoiding something. Worth examining honestly.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2021**: Lenny "On taking time off" + "Three myths about sabbatical programs" + "How to create an exceptional coverage plan for parental leave" — foundational corpus content.
- **2024-2026**: Multiple "taking the week off" newsletter entries; corpus modeling.
- **2026**: AI-tooling time-off-as-skill-building inflection emerges.
