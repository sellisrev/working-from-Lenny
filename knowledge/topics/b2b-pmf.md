# Finding product-market fit in B2B

## Current consensus

B2B PMF differs from consumer PMF in fundamental ways. The corpus consensus, anchored in Lenny's "Guide for finding product-market fit in B2B" (2023) plus Todd Jackson's First Round PMF framework (2024):

### B2B PMF signals
1. **Customers pull the product out of you.** Inbound > outbound; users champion you internally without prompting.
2. **5-10 customers in the same segment paying full price** is the threshold most corpus operators cite. Not 50 random customers; the same shape of customer.
3. **Reference willingness.** Are customers willing to publicly reference you? In B2B, this is the strongest single signal.
4. **Word-of-mouth growth.** New deals come unsolicited from existing customers' networks.
5. **Net dollar retention > 100%** within the target segment (cf. [churn-and-payback](churn-and-payback.md)). Existing accounts grow.

### Differences from consumer PMF
- **Long sales cycles distort retention metrics.** A B2B customer might "retain" because of a multi-year contract while actively churning in usage.
- **Multiple personas matter.** Buyer (signs the check), user (uses the product), influencer (champions internally) — PMF requires all three to align.
- **Single customer can drag the team off-track.** A whale's specific demands can pull product roadmap in directions that don't fit the broader market. Push back.

### Common failure modes
- **Premature scaling**: hiring sales before PMF locks in. Cf. [hiring-early-team](hiring-early-team.md).
- **Confusing pipeline with PMF.** A pipeline of "interested" prospects who don't close is not PMF.
- **Single-segment PMF mistaken for universal PMF.** Many B2B companies have PMF for SMB but not enterprise (or vice versa). Expansion failure is common.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-native B2B compresses PMF timeline.** Some AI B2B products (Cursor, Lovable for B2B-adjacent use) reach PMF in months not years.
- **Outcome-based pricing as PMF signal.** When customers willingly accept outcome-based pricing, you have unusually strong PMF (they're willing to bet on results, not just promise to try).

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO pre-PMF**: don't hire sales beyond founder-led until you have 5-10 same-segment customers paying full price.
- **If you're an investor evaluating a B2B Series A**: ask for the segment definition + 5-10-customer reference list. "We have happy customers" without segment specificity is weak.
- **If you're a sales lead joining a pre-PMF B2B**: the founder should still own most relationships; you're scaling a known motion, not finding one.
- **If you're a product lead at a multi-segment B2B**: track PMF by segment, not in aggregate. Segments diverge.
