# Restructuring orgs for the AI era

## Current consensus

Companies are restructuring around AI-era reality, not just adopting AI tools within existing structures. Three distinct restructuring patterns have emerged in the corpus:

### 1. Fast-thinking + slow-thinking dual structure (Howie Liu / Airtable)

Howie Liu (Aug 2025) restructured Airtable around the explicit Kahneman dual-system metaphor:

- **Fast-thinking group** ("AI Platform"): ships major new capability ~weekly. Operates like the Cursor/Lovable companies — drop-jaw new features, near-zero coordination tax.
- **Slow-thinking group**: deliberate, premeditated work like infrastructure changes (HyperDB), enterprise scale, etc. Cannot be hacked into a one-week prototype.

Liu's logic: feature-team or business-unit org structures don't allow AI-native shipping pace because they impose roadmap separation. AI-native companies look like one cohesive product shipping at breakneck pace, not separate roadmaps. The fast/slow split preserves ambitious infra work without slowing the AI-native cadence.

### 2. Product-as-organism (Asha Sharma / Microsoft AI Platform)

Sharma (Aug 2025): the role of products is shifting from "artifacts you ship" to "organisms that ingest data, digest reward signals, and create outcomes." The KPI shift: "what is the metabolism of a product team to be able to ingest data and digest the rewards model?"

Implication for org structure: every product team needs a closed-loop measurement-and-iteration capability. Teams without this become the bottleneck of the company's broader AI ambitions.

### 3. AI-first hiring with massive shedding-and-rehiring cycle (Singhal forecast)

Nikhyl Singhal (April 2026): companies will shed 30k and hire 8k AI-first within 12-24 months. The 8k are predominantly builders, not "information mover" PMs. Cf. [ai-replacing-pms](ai-replacing-pms.md), [hiring-pms-as-non-pm](hiring-pms-as-non-pm.md). This is the most aggressive structural prediction in the corpus and is contested but widely cited.

### Common cross-cutting principles

- **Skills are merging across roles.** Cat Wu / Anthropic: PMs do engineering work, engineers do PM work, designers PM and ship code. Org structures that enforce strict role boundaries lose to ones that don't.
- **Agents will outnumber employees by ~2026.** Asha Sharma's framing. Consequence: org charts need to account for agent capacity, not just headcount.
- **The IC CEO is back.** Lenny in conversation with Howie Liu: "CEOs almost becoming individual contributors again, getting into the code, building things, leading initiatives themselves." Founder-mode + AI-tooling = founders shipping their own code in production. Especially common at Series A-B AI companies.
- **All-in or don't.** Brian Scanlan / Intercom: half-measures don't work. The companies seeing 2x throughput went all-in on a single coding agent (Claude Code) and built a full skills + telemetry + governance stack around it.
- **Hire to AI-first design from day zero.** Howie Liu: "If you were literally founding a new company from scratch with the same mission, how would you execute on that mission using a fully AI native approach? If you can't, then you should find a buyer." This is harsh but reflects the operating reality at AI-native competitors.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a CEO or founder**: schedule a working session with leadership to ask Howie Liu's question — "if we were founding from scratch today, AI-native, what would we do?" The gap between the answer and your current org is your restructuring agenda.
- **If you're a board member**: ask portfolio CEOs about org structure ↔ AI strategy alignment. A leadership team that hasn't restructured anything in the last 18 months is probably underperforming on AI execution.
- **If you're an HR / People leader**: legacy "level / role / job-family" structures are increasingly bad fits. Title compression, pay-band restructuring, and merged role definitions (e.g., "engineering builder" rather than separate IC1-IC4 ladders) are early experiments. Watch Lovable, Cursor, Linear for emerging patterns.
- **If you're a senior engineering manager**: the 1:1 PM-to-engineering-team ratio is dissolving at AI-native companies. Your team may need fewer PMs, or different PMs (builders, not coordinators). Coordinate with PM leadership to redesign the boundary.
- **If you're a junior employee at a non-AI-native company**: the structure may be slow to change. The leverage you have personally is to demonstrate AI-augmented productivity within your existing role, even if the org doesn't restructure around you.

## How thinking has evolved

Earliest takes:

- **2023-2024**: corpus mostly silent on AI-driven org restructuring. Discussion confined to "should we add a Head of AI?" type questions.

Middle period:

- **2025-08-28**: Asha Sharma (Microsoft AI Platform) — first major framing of "product as organism" and "death of org charts." Predicts agents outnumber employees by 2026. ([Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J9UWaltU-7Q))

- **2025-08-31**: Howie Liu (Airtable) — concrete case study of restructuring Airtable's whole org. Fast-thinking + slow-thinking split. IC CEO emergence. ([Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GT0jtVjRy2E))

Recent takes:

- **2026-04-19**: Nikhyl Singhal — most aggressive structural forecast. 30k shed / 8k AI-first hire dynamics ahead. Builders wanted. ([Lenny's Podcast](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-half-of-product-managers-are-in-trouble))

- **2026-04-23**: Cat Wu — Anthropic's own org as a model: skills merge across roles, lightweight PM model, fast research previews, engineers shipping end-to-end. ([Lenny's Podcast](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-anthropic-product-team-moves-faster))

External current consensus (web, weighted recent + AI-aware):

- a16z, Sequoia, and BCG have all published 2025-2026 essays on AI-era org structures. Convergent: smaller teams shipping more; flatter hierarchies; higher per-employee leverage.
- Klarna's "We don't hire" memo (which they later partially walked back) became the canonical extreme case of org-restructuring-around-AI rhetoric. The corpus does not endorse this extreme.

## Inflection points

- **Asha Sharma "products as organisms" framing (Aug 2025)**: corpus formalization of the post-artifact product paradigm.
- **Howie Liu's Airtable reorg (mid-2025)**: first detailed CEO case study of restructuring around AI.
- **Sonnet 4.6 / Opus 4.6 (Nov-Dec 2025)**: model release that flipped many organizations from "experimenting" to "all-in" mode (per Brian Scanlan); structural changes accelerate after this point.
- **Nikhyl Singhal "shed 30k, hire 8k AI-first" forecast (April 2026)**: most aggressive structural forecast in the corpus to date.

## Open questions

- Does the fast-thinking + slow-thinking split generalize beyond Airtable's specific data + AI use case? Howie Liu's answer is yes; not yet proven.
- For very large organizations (Microsoft, Google, Apple): can structural agility match an AI-native startup, or is structural inertia an unfixable disadvantage? Cat Wu's Anthropic example is a startup-scale answer; large-company answers are TBD.
- For regulated industries (financial services, health care): how much structural restructuring is even legal/compliant? The corpus has not addressed this directly.
- Does the IC CEO pattern compromise CEO governance functions? Lenny notes the trend without resolving the tradeoff.

## Cross-corpus reinforcement (How I AI as secondary signal)

How I AI episodes consistently feature CEOs / senior leaders shipping code (Eric Simons at StackBlitz, Anton Osika at Lovable, Howie Liu, Drew Houston at Dropbox during dogfooding) — empirical reinforcement of the IC CEO framing. **Reinforces** the main-corpus claim about role merging and fast-iteration culture without contradicting on any major point.
