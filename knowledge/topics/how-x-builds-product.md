# How <company> builds product (case study lessons)

A meta-topic. The corpus contains a sustained "How X builds product" series — Notion, Figma, Duolingo, Coda, Miro, Ramp, Shopify, Snowflake, Linear, Perplexity, Gong, and more. Each is a discrete case study; this topic indexes the recurring patterns.

## Current consensus: cross-company patterns that recur

### Pattern 1: founder / CPO operates close to the product
Across Notion (Ivan Zhao), Figma (Yuhki Yamashata, Dylan Field), Duolingo, Linear, Coda (Shishir Mehrotra), Ramp (Geoff Charles): founders / CPOs are hands-on, not detached. Cf. [founder-mode](founder-mode.md), [ai-org-restructuring](ai-org-restructuring.md).

### Pattern 2: small empowered teams
Most companies in the series operate small cross-functional teams (5-10 people) with end-to-end ownership of an area. Cf. [working-with-pms-as-engineer](working-with-pms-as-engineer.md), Cagan's Empowered model.

### Pattern 3: customer obsession is operationalized
Not "we care about customers" rhetoric; specific practices — Notion's community management, Figma's user research depth, Linear's customer-feedback-to-changelog cycle.

### Pattern 4: design quality is non-negotiable
Linear, Notion, Stripe, Figma all invested in design at scale beyond what their stage justified. Cf. [product-design-craft](product-design-craft.md).

### Pattern 5: opinionated product strategy
Each successful company in the series has a clear opinion about how their category should work. Vague positioning correlates with weak outcomes; sharp positioning correlates with category-king status.

### Pattern 6: shipping cadence is a culture choice
Shopify (Farhan Thawar high-intensity culture), Linear (taste + velocity), Ramp (velocity over everything), Anthropic Claude Code (1-2 week research previews) — all chose pace deliberately.

### Pattern 7 (2026 emergence): AI tooling adoption is operational priority
Asha Sharma at Microsoft, Howie Liu at Airtable, Brian Scanlan at Intercom — corpus 2026 examples explicitly restructure around AI. Cf. [ai-org-restructuring](ai-org-restructuring.md).

## How to use case studies

- **Don't copy practices in isolation.** Each company's practices fit their context (stage, market, team).
- **Pattern-match across multiple companies.** When 5 companies in different categories converge on a pattern, that's signal.
- **Be skeptical of single-company prescriptions.** What worked at Stripe might not work at your startup.
- **Look for what they don't do.** Conspicuous absences (e.g., Linear not running OKRs) are as informative as practices.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an exec considering "how should we build product?"**: read 5+ case studies in your category before locking in practices.
- **If you're a founder pre-PMF**: case studies of post-PMF companies are aspirational; pre-PMF case studies (Lovable, Cursor, Bolt's pivots) are more relevant.
- **If you're an investor**: this corpus thread is one of the highest-density operational learning sources. Track it.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2022-2024**: Lenny corpus accumulates 20+ "How X builds product" episodes across companies and stages.
- **2024-2026**: AI-tooling adoption becomes a consistent thread across recent case studies.
