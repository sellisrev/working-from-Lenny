# Raising a seed round

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "Raising a seed round 101" (2024) + Jessica Livingston (Y Combinator) + Dalton Caldwell (YC, 2024) + Jason Fried (37signals contrarian).

### The mechanics
1. **Pick the right investors, not the most prominent.** Investors who'll be useful at this stage > investors with the biggest brand.
2. **Warm intros beat cold pitches.** 90%+ of seed rounds close via referral.
3. **The "round" is a manufactured event.** Most seeds happen as a series of single conversations until a lead emerges; party-round dynamics happen at companies with real momentum.
4. **The deck matters less than the conversation.** Be ready for both.
5. **Optimize for the right amount of money, not the maximum.** Too much capital before you know what you're doing leads to expensive false starts.

### What investors look for at seed
- **Founder quality** dominates at seed. Often more about the founder than the idea.
- **Team density.** Co-founder fit signals durability through the inevitable hard times.
- **Validation evidence.** Customer conversations, pilots, LOIs.
- **Market inflection.** Mike Maples Jr. framing: investors look for inflection-driven opportunities.
- **AI-fluency in 2026.** Founders who can't explain their AI thesis at seed will struggle.

### The Jason Fried (37signals) contrarian take
- **You don't have to raise.** Bootstrapping is a viable path; many great companies skip the venture path.
- **Venture funding has costs:** dilution, time spent fundraising, pressure for hypergrowth, board governance. Calibrate.
- **Profitable from day one** is a defensible strategy in many markets.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI seed rounds are larger and faster.** AI-native companies often raise $5-15M seed rounds with one or two customer letters; the bar has shifted.
- **Distribution-as-moat** (Spiegel framing) is a credible seed pitch in some categories.
- **Faster YC + accelerator path** to seed is increasingly common.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a first-time founder**: read Lenny's Raising a seed round 101 + listen to Jessica Livingston / Dalton Caldwell episodes. Then read Jason Fried for balance.
- **If you're an angel investor**: most YC + first-time founder rounds are oversubscribed; getting access matters as much as picking.
- **If you're an existing employee considering joining a seed-stage startup**: ask for the data room (if you can). Many seed-stage startups don't survive the first 3 years.
