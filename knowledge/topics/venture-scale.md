# Venture-scale ideas (and which aren't)

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "Your startup idea probably isn't venture-scale" (2024) + Andrew Wilkinson "you're probably chasing the wrong idea" (2025) + Mike Maples Jr. on Pattern Breakers + Dalton Caldwell (YC) on tar pit ideas.

### What "venture-scale" means
- **$1B+ outcome path.** Investors model returns assuming the winner returns 10-100x their fund. A $50M acquisition isn't venture-scale.
- **Large addressable market.** TAM matters; $100M markets can't return venture funds.
- **Defensible moat.** Cf. [seven-powers](seven-powers.md). Without a power, the business gets commoditized.
- **Acceptable timeline.** Venture funds typically need 7-10 year exits.

### Most ideas aren't venture-scale
- Most consumer apps. Even good ones.
- Most consulting / services businesses.
- Most marketplace ideas (cf. Andrew Chen *Cold Start Problem*).
- Most "X for Y" ideas (Uber for laundry, etc.) — small markets.

### The "tar pit" anti-pattern (Dalton Caldwell, YC)
- **Tar pit ideas** look attractive but trap founders for years without breakthrough.
- Common tar pits: marketplaces with low frequency / low value, social products in saturated categories, B2B in commoditized segments.

### Mike Maples Jr.'s pattern-breakers framing
- **Inflection-driven ideas** are the best venture-scale candidates. Without a market inflection, you compete in stable markets where incumbents win.
- **Insights, not inspirations.** Look for non-obvious truths the market hasn't internalized yet.

### Andrew Wilkinson "you're chasing the wrong idea"
- Even at scale, picking ideas is hard.
- Most ideas are wrong; resilience to abandon them is essential.
- Some ideas are tar pits even when they look obvious.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI shifts what "venture-scale" looks like.** Some traditionally-non-venture-scale categories (e.g., legal services automation, accounting automation) are now venture-scale because AI changes the unit economics.
- **AI infrastructure plays** (Cursor, Anthropic, Lovable) demonstrated that AI-tooling categories can scale faster than any prior tech category.
- **The "tar pit" detection question** for AI startups: is your moat the foundation model access, or do you build something on top? Foundation-model-dependent is a tar pit if Anthropic/OpenAI reproduces the feature.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a founder considering an idea**: ask honestly whether it's venture-scale. If not, bootstrapping may be the better path. Cf. [bootstrapping](bootstrapping.md).
- **If you're an angel/investor**: avoid tar pits. Pattern-recognition matters here.
- **If you're an aspiring founder reading "follow your passion"**: venture-scale ideas are usually about market structure, not founder passion. The two need to align.
