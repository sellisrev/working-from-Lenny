# Increasing virality and consumer-app virality

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "How to increase virality" (2021) + "Virality is a myth (mostly)" counter-take + Nikita Bier's playbook (TBH / Gas, 2024) + Lessons on building a viral consumer app: the story of Saturn (2023).

### The reality
- **Virality is real but rare.** Most products that try to go viral don't.
- **Built-in virality > campaign virality.** The product itself spreads users vs. asking users to invite.
- **Network effects take a few years to compound.** Even successful viral products usually have 12-36 month arcs to mass adoption.

### Lenny's "Virality is a myth (mostly)" counter-take
- **Most "viral" success stories had distribution + network effects + product magic, not just word-of-mouth.**
- **Inviting friends is high-friction.** Don't bet on this as the primary mechanic.
- **Built-in virality (artifacts users share, network features) beats explicit invites.**

### Nikita Bier's consumer playbook
- **High-school / college targeting.** Concentrated communities; rapid spread.
- **Specific psychological hook.** Not "all things to all people"; one tight emotional hook.
- **Dense distribution.** Get to 30%+ of one community before expanding.
- **Cf. atomic network framing** (Andrew Chen).

### Saturn's viral case study
- **High-school scheduling app + social layer.** Specific tight use case + network mechanics.
- **Compounding through density.** One school adopts → next school adopts → etc.
- **Burn-out cliff.** Many viral consumer apps peak then decline; sustaining requires habit-formation (cf. [retention](retention.md)).

### Common failure modes
- **Asking for invites.** Most users won't.
- **Going broad too fast.** Pre-density expansion dilutes; consolidates fail.
- **Building for influencer launches.** Influencer spikes don't produce durable user bases.
- **Confusing initial viral spike with PMF.** Cf. [product-market-fit](product-market-fit.md). Spike + churn ≠ PMF.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI-generated viral artifacts** (Lovable apps, ChatGPT shares) generate built-in virality through the artifacts themselves.
- **The "vibe-coded thing I made" share is a new viral primitive.** Cf. Lenny "What people are vibe coding" — many vibe-coded apps spread via their own existence.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a marketer planning a viral campaign**: don't. Build product-level virality into the product instead.
- **If you're a founder of a consumer startup**: Nikita Bier's playbook + Cold Start Problem (Andrew Chen) are essential reading.
- **If you're an investor evaluating a "we'll go viral" pitch**: probe for the mechanism. Generic virality claims are weak; specific artifact-spreads-to-non-users mechanisms are strong.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2021-2022**: Lenny "How to increase virality" + "Virality is a myth (mostly)" — corpus-canonical.
- **2022**: Saturn case study; Nikita Bier consumer playbook.
- **2024-2025**: AI-generated artifacts as viral primitives.
