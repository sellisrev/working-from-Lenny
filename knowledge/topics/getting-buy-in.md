# Getting buy-in

## Current consensus

Getting buy-in is the practice of moving a proposal from "your idea" to "our decision." Corpus consensus:

### The progression
1. **One-on-one socializing first.** Don't unveil a major proposal in a group meeting. Test it 1:1 with key stakeholders; refine; THEN bring to group.
2. **Address objections preemptively.** Document what you expect people to push back on; have answers ready.
3. **Pre-mortem the proposal.** "If this fails, why?" Surface concerns before someone else does.
4. **Make the recommendation explicit.** "I recommend we do X because Y" beats "we should consider X."
5. **Use evidence proportionate to stakes.** Big decisions need data; small decisions get bogged down by data overhead.

### The political reality
- **The decision-maker is rarely your audience alone.** Stakeholders, peers, your boss, their boss — each may need a tailored conversation.
- **First mover often wins.** Whoever frames the problem usually shapes the solution.
- **Allies matter.** A single supporter beats no supporters; three coordinated supporters often carry the room.

### The wrong way to get buy-in
- **Surprise meetings.** Big proposals to ungroomed audiences guarantee defensive reactions.
- **Browbeating.** "Just trust me" doesn't work and damages future credibility.
- **Building consensus on everything.** Some decisions you can't get unanimous support on; don't try.
- **Calling for vote when you should be making the call.** Some things are yours to decide; voting them surrenders authority.

### The Wes Kao addition (2024)
Wes Kao's communication craft: anchor the recommendation in a "because" structure that connects to the audience's existing priorities. "I recommend X because it serves our quarterly goal Y."

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **Prototypes substitute for slide decks.** Cf. Aparna Chennapragada / Cat Wu. A working clickable demo carries more buy-in than a 30-slide narrative.
- **Async writing carries more buy-in than meetings** at AI-native companies. Document the proposal; let people read; then meet to discuss.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineer with a technical proposal**: same playbook. Pre-socialize; address objections; use working code as evidence.
- **If you're a manager whose team isn't getting buy-in for ideas**: probably a process issue, not idea quality. Coach the team on the playbook.
- **If you're a CEO trying to align leadership**: 1:1 socialization > all-hands announcement. Your VPs need to feel ownership.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2020-2022**: Lenny "Getting buy-in" + "Defending your big bets" foundational.
- **2024**: Wes Kao on communication-as-influence; Carolyn Robin on relationships.
- **2025-2026**: Prototype-driven buy-in becomes table stakes.
