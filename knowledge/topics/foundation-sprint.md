# Foundation Sprint and Design Sprint

## Current consensus

Two related but distinct frameworks from Jake Knapp & John Zeratsky (Sprint, Make Time, Character Capital):

### Design Sprint (2016 origin, still canonical for product-shape questions)
A 5-day structured workshop to take a tough problem from idea to validated prototype. Five steps: understand → diverge → decide → prototype → test. Used widely at Google Ventures and elsewhere.

### Foundation Sprint (2024-2025 introduction)
A 2-day structured workshop for *earlier* questions: who's the customer? what's the problem? what's the solution direction? Earlier-stage than Design Sprint; designed for pre-PMF startups crystallizing their first version of strategy.

The corpus consensus:

- **Foundation Sprint is for "we don't know what we're building yet."** Pre-PMF, pre-strategy. Two-day workshop to align the team on the foundational answers.
- **Design Sprint is for "we know what we're building, but not how."** Post-strategy, pre-launch. Five-day workshop to validate a specific product approach.
- **Both work best with cross-functional teams.** Engineer + designer + PM + founder, in a room (physical or virtual), for the duration. Asynchronous variants exist but compromise the framework.
- **Both produce artifacts.** A Foundation Sprint produces a written strategic foundation; a Design Sprint produces a tested prototype. Without artifacts, the workshop was theater.

## What's changed in 2025-2026

- **AI-augmented sprints.** AI prototyping (cf. [ai-prototyping](ai-prototyping.md)) collapses the prototype day of a Design Sprint from full-day to half-day. The understanding / diverge / decide phases haven't changed.
- **Foundation Sprint adoption is rising for AI-native startups.** The underlying questions (who's the customer? what's the problem?) are arguably *more* important when the technology is fast-changing because it's tempting to skip them.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a founder pre-PMF**: a Foundation Sprint is the highest-leverage way to align the team on what you're actually building. Don't skip it because "we already know."
- **If you're an exec sponsoring a new product initiative**: book a Foundation Sprint before the team commits to a roadmap. The cost is 2 days of leadership time; the avoided cost is 6 months of building the wrong thing.
- **If you're a designer or engineer being asked to participate**: do it. Don't push back as "another offsite." The structured nature of the Sprint is what makes it productive.
- **If you're a board member**: a portfolio company that hasn't done a Foundation Sprint or equivalent for a major new initiative is probably under-invested in pre-execution alignment.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2016**: Knapp & Zeratsky publish *Sprint*. Design Sprint becomes industry-standard.
- **2024-2025**: Knapp & Zeratsky introduce Foundation Sprint as the earlier-stage complement.
- **2024-2025**: Lenny's "Introducing the Foundation Sprint" newsletter documents the framework for the corpus.
