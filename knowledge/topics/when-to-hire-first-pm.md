# When to hire your first PM

## Current consensus

Anchored in Lenny "When to hire your first product manager" (2021, foundational) + "When they hired their first PM" (2023, retrospective on real companies) + AI-era updates (Cat Wu, Singhal).

### The corpus consensus signal: later than founders think
- **Most successful companies hired their first PM after 50+ employees.** Founder-led product strategy + founder-led customer conversations work for surprisingly long.
- **Hire when the founder becomes the bottleneck**, not when the company "should have a PM."
- **Engineers + designers can do PM-shaped work** for a long time. Cf. [understanding-pm-role-as-non-pm](understanding-pm-role-as-non-pm.md).

### The signs you might need a PM
- **Founder is doing PM work but resenting it.** When the founder is good at PM but doesn't want to do it forever.
- **Cross-functional coordination is breaking.** Sales asks not getting prioritized; engineering decisions made without strategic context.
- **Customer-discovery work is dropping off.** No one is consistently talking to users.
- **Roadmap is unclear or reactive.** Team doesn't know what's coming or why.

### The signs you don't need a PM yet
- **No PMF.** Cf. [product-market-fit](product-market-fit.md). PMs don't find PMF — founders do, with engineers and designers.
- **Small team (< 10 employees).** Coordination overhead doesn't justify the role.
- **Founder still has bandwidth for product strategy + customer work.**

### The 2026 update (Cat Wu / Singhal era)
- **Engineers with product taste substitute for early PMs at AI-native companies.** Cat Wu's hiring filter at Anthropic.
- **Information-mover PM as first hire is the wrong PM.** Cf. [ai-replacing-pms](ai-replacing-pms.md). Hire builders, not coordinators.
- **The founder-as-IC-CEO** pattern (cf. [founder-mode](founder-mode.md), [ai-org-restructuring](ai-org-restructuring.md)) extends the runway before a PM is needed.

### Common failure modes
- **Hiring a PM as a status signal.** "Real companies have PMs" isn't a hiring rationale.
- **Hiring a senior PM too early.** Senior PMs need a working playbook to lead; without one, they struggle.
- **Hiring an APM as the first PM.** APMs need mentorship; the founder can't be the mentor in this case.
- **Hiring before establishing the role.** "We'll figure out what they should do once they're here" produces frustrated PMs.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a founder**: stretch the founder-led product phase as long as possible. Most successful companies waited longer than they realized they needed to.
- **If you're an engineering lead at an early-stage company**: you may be doing PM work without the title. Negotiate the title as the company scales.
- **If you're a sales lead at a startup pre-PMF**: don't pressure the founder to hire a PM to "fix" sales-product alignment. Pre-PMF is the founder's domain.
- **If you're a candidate considering joining as the first PM**: cf. Lenny "Joining as the first product manager" (2020). It's a specific challenge; not all PMs thrive in this context.
