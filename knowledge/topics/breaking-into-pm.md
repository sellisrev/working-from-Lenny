# Breaking into product management

## Current consensus

The 2026 path into PM is meaningfully different from 2022. The consensus:

### Durable principles (still hold)
1. **Internal transitions are easier than external entries.** "Become a PM at your current company" is a higher-success-rate path than "become a PM somewhere new."
2. **Adjacent-function origins work best.** Engineering, design, customer success, ops — all viable PM entry points.
3. **Ship something visible.** Internal product launches, side projects, open-source contributions all count.
4. **Build the network.** Most PM hiring is through referrals; cultivate the network before you need it.
5. **Pass the interview.** Cf. [pm-interviews](pm-interviews.md). The interview is its own skill.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
6. **The "I want to be a PM" pitch alone is now weak.** Singhal: builders wanted, not aspirants. Bring evidence — a vibe-coded prototype, a shipped feature, a business outcome. A 2026 candidate who can demo a working prototype they built has an enormous advantage over one who has only career stories.
7. **AI tools change the entry calculus.** A non-engineer can now build prototypes (Cursor, Bolt, Lovable). This expands the candidate pool for PM-shaped roles to include people who couldn't have demonstrated product taste pre-2024.
8. **Information-mover PM is no longer a viable entry archetype.** Cf. [ai-replacing-pms](ai-replacing-pms.md). Don't market yourself as "the person who runs the standup"; market yourself as "the person who shipped X."

### Common failure modes
- Treating PM as "manager of engineers." It's not.
- Assuming an MBA is the path. Sometimes useful; not necessary.
- Reading PM books without shipping anything. The reading is necessary but insufficient.
- Applying to senior PM roles cold without product experience. The PM-to-PM lateral hiring market is much more permeable than the entry-level external market.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an engineer wanting to become a PM**: build product taste publicly. Open-source projects, technical blog posts about product decisions, side products. Internal transitions follow.
- **If you're a designer wanting to transition**: same playbook plus emphasizing your existing user-research/prototyping skills.
- **If you're a customer success / sales / ops lead**: emphasize your customer empathy and direct user contact. These are durable assets in a builder PM hire.
- **If you're a hiring manager evaluating a PM candidate from an adjacent function**: weight shipped product evidence heavily; weight pure career stories less.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2019-2021**: Lenny "How to get into product management," "Should I become a product manager," "Transitioning from DS to PM" — foundational.
- **2022**: Lenny "Skills PMs need to build."
- **2024-2026**: AI-era reframing — builders wanted; demo-ship something to differentiate.
