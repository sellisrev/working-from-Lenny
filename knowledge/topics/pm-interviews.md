# PM interviews and interview questions (candidate side)

For the hiring-manager side, see [hiring-pms-as-non-pm](hiring-pms-as-non-pm.md). This topic is the candidate-prep complement.

## Current consensus

The corpus has converged on a small set of question categories that recur across companies. Prepare for these; the specifics vary by company but the categories are stable.

### The five canonical question categories (Lenny + corpus consensus)
1. **Collaboration**: tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineer/designer and how you resolved it.
2. **Execution**: pick a 3-9 month project; walk me through it end-to-end.
3. **Strategy**: pick a product you worked on; talk me through your strategy for it.
4. **Customer insights**: tell me about user research that had a big impact.
5. **Impact**: most important product you shipped — what made it impactful, would it have happened without you?

### The "single best interview question" tradition (Lenny, 2024)
25 questions from 150 podcast guests. Patterns:
- "What's the hardest decision you've made?"
- "What did you learn from your biggest failure?"
- "Tell me about a time you changed your mind based on data."
The good ones probe judgment, self-awareness, and learning rate.

### Product sense interviews
Cf. [product-sense](product-sense.md). The "definitive guide to mastering product sense interviews" (Lenny, 2025): structured frameworks for breaking down product critique questions.
- User → problem → goal → solution → tradeoffs → metrics.
- Not memorizing one framework; bringing the right one to the right question.

### The take-home / project portion
Lenny's emphasis: project portion is the most informative part of any process. As a candidate, treat it seriously; as a hiring panelist, don't skip it.

### Common candidate failure modes
- **Underprepared on the company.** Read recent press, customer reviews, financial filings if public, current product.
- **Generic answers.** "I led the team to alignment" without specifics. Concrete numbers, dates, decisions.
- **No questions for the interviewer.** Always have 3-5 thoughtful questions ready.
- **Pretending product sense.** If you don't use the product, say so honestly; ask what surprised them.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **AI tooling demos are now common.** Bring a vibe-coded prototype. Cat Wu / Anthropic and similar companies actively look for this.
- **The "info-mover PM" archetype is no longer a winning interview narrative.** Lead with your shipped work, not your meeting facilitation. Cf. [ai-replacing-pms](ai-replacing-pms.md).

## What this means for non-PM candidates considering a PM role

- **If you're an engineer interviewing for PM roles**: your shipped engineering work + product taste is your strongest pitch. Frame the work as "I made these product decisions, not just executed them."
- **If you're a designer**: your design portfolio plus user research stories translate cleanly. Frame UX decisions as product decisions.
- **If you're a sales / GTM person**: your customer conversations are PM gold. Pull specific customer-need-to-product-decision stories.
