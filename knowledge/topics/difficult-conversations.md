# Difficult conversations and feedback

## Current consensus

For the manager-side / feedback-giving view, see [giving-feedback-as-leader](giving-feedback-as-leader.md). This is the broader cross-context view: hard conversations of any direction (peer, up, down, customer, board).

### Key frameworks in the corpus
- **Radical Candor (Kim Scott)**: care personally + challenge directly. The 2x2 of caring vs. challenging maps every difficult conversation.
- **GAIN framework (Therese Huston / Lenny 2025)**: Goal + Ask + Impact + Next steps. Evidence-based; engineered to maximize what's heard.
- **Alisa Cohn's scripts (2024-12)**: specific verbal patterns for hard conversations, navigating defensiveness, ending meetings well.
- **Kenneth Berger's "asking for what you want"** (2024): the under-discussed difficult conversation is the one where you ask for what you actually need.
- **Rachel Lockett's high-trust framing (2025)**: conversations between people with established trust go differently — invest in trust before you need to have hard conversations.

### What works across contexts
1. **Schedule the conversation.** Don't ambush. Both parties need cognitive bandwidth.
2. **Lead with care, not the problem.** "I want this to work" / "I value our partnership" before the hard part.
3. **Be specific.** "When X happened, Y impact" beats "you're not collaborating."
4. **Listen for understanding before responding.** Most defensive reactions come from feeling unheard.
5. **End with explicit agreement.** What changes? Who does what by when? Without this the conversation produces nothing.

### What doesn't work
- **Email / Slack for hard conversations.** Tone flattens; misreads escalate.
- **Surprise feedback in front of others.** Public criticism + private praise is the durable pattern.
- **"Just be direct" without care.** Cf. Kim Scott's "obnoxious aggression" quadrant.
- **Avoidance.** Most difficult conversations get harder when delayed.

### Common failure modes
- **Ruinous empathy** (Kim Scott): caring without challenging produces unfixed problems.
- **Sandwich feedback** (positive-negative-positive): widely taught; widely transparent; rarely effective.
- **Going too high.** Skip-level conversations should usually go through your manager first.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **Async tools** make it tempting to send hard feedback in writing. Don't. Schedule the synchronous conversation.
- **Rachel Lockett (2025) on high-trust teams**: cultural frame for getting better at this collectively, not individual-skill alone.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a manager avoiding hard feedback**: Kim Scott's "ruinous empathy" diagnosis is yours. Read *Radical Candor*; practice; iterate.
- **If you're an IC dreading a conversation with your manager**: Kenneth Berger's "asking for what you want" is the framework. Be direct about your need.
- **If you're a peer dealing with a difficult colleague**: the corpus has multiple resources (Alisa Cohn, Joe Hudson, Rachel Lockett).
- **If you're a CEO**: model the practice. Hard conversations downstream depend on your example.

## How thinking has evolved

- **2017-2024**: Kim Scott *Radical Candor* canon (book + Lenny episode 2024).
- **2024**: Kenneth Berger on asking for what you want; Carolyn Robin on relationships.
- **2024-12**: Alisa Cohn scripts.
- **2025**: Therese Huston GAIN framework + Rachel Lockett high-trust framing.
