# Communicating bad news

## Current consensus

Lenny "Communicating bad news" (2020 — Issue 26, foundational) + Alisa Cohn 2024 scripts + Carolyn Robin relationship-tested-in-bad-news framing.

### The principles
1. **Tell people quickly.** Delaying bad news compounds the damage; people resent finding out late.
2. **Be specific.** Vague bad news ("things aren't going great") is worse than specific bad news ("we're missing Q1 revenue by 15%").
3. **Lead with what changes.** Practical impact first; context after.
4. **Take responsibility where appropriate.** Don't deflect.
5. **Have a plan.** Bad news + "and here's what we're doing" lands better than bad news alone.

### What kind of bad news
- **Layoffs / restructuring.** Cf. [layoffs-prep](layoffs-prep.md) and Matt Mochary "fire people with grace."
- **Missed targets / pivots.** Cf. [pivots-art](pivots-art.md).
- **Personal failure / accountability.** Owning your own mistake matters more than spinning it.
- **Customer issues / outages.** Be transparent; over-communicate.

### Common failure modes
- **Sandwich method.** Positive-negative-positive comes across as manipulation.
- **Hiding it.** People find out anyway; the trust damage from hiding is permanent.
- **Over-explaining.** Bad news + 10 minutes of justification reads as guilt.
- **Emotional unpreparedness.** If you can't deliver the news without breaking, prepare more before delivering.

### Alisa Cohn's scripts (2024)
- Specific verbal patterns for common bad-news scenarios.
- Pre-prepared scripts let you be present in the moment rather than scrambling for words.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **Async written bad news is increasingly common** (memos, all-hands docs). It's worse than synchronous for hard news; reserve async for medium-stakes communication.
- **Founder-as-direct-channel** (cf. [pr-and-press](pr-and-press.md)) makes bad-news communication more visible than in pre-social-media era.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're a manager about to deliver bad news**: prepare the words. Scripts work. Don't wing it.
- **If you're a CEO communicating layoffs / pivots**: speed + specificity + own-responsibility + plan. All four.
- **If you're an exec receiving bad news from a report**: thank them for telling you. Don't punish the messenger; you'll get less information later.
