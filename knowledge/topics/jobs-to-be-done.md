# Jobs-to-be-done framework

## Current consensus

Bob Moesta's JTBD framework (co-creator with Clayton Christensen) is corpus-canonical. The model: customers "hire" products to do specific "jobs" in their lives. Understanding the job — not the customer demographic — drives product decisions.

### The core questions
1. **What's the job the customer is hiring this product for?** (Functional + emotional + social dimensions.)
2. **What were they doing before?** (Including non-purchase: hacking around, doing without, doing it manually.)
3. **What forces drove them to switch?** (Push from current solution; pull of new solution; anxiety / habit anchors.)
4. **What does success look like for them?** (Beyond just buying the product.)

### Bob Moesta's switch interview methodology
Detailed conversational structure for understanding why a specific customer made a specific switch. Reveals the actual job; surfaces the forces. The corpus-canonical episode (Bob Moesta, 2024-01-04) walks through the methodology.

### How JTBD differs from personas / segments
- **Personas describe the customer.** JTBD describes what the customer is trying to accomplish.
- **Segments group customers by attribute.** JTBD groups customers by job.
- **The same customer hires different products for different jobs.** A single user can be in many JTBD groups simultaneously.

### Why JTBD wins for product decisions
- **Stable across demographic shifts.** Demographics evolve; jobs are durable.
- **Surfaces non-obvious competitors.** McDonald's milkshake vs. boredom in commute (Christensen's classic): the actual competitor wasn't another milkshake.
- **Translates directly to product specs.** Once you know the job, you know what the product needs to do.

### What's changed in 2025-2026
- **JTBD applies cleanly to AI products.** Users hire AI tools for specific jobs ("write better PRDs", "ship code faster", "summarize meetings"). The framework is durable.
- **AI tools change the JTBD landscape.** New jobs emerge ("prototype an idea overnight"); some old jobs collapse ("hire someone to research X for me"). JTBD analysis reveals these shifts before traditional metrics do.

## What this means for non-PM consumers

- **If you're an exec evaluating a product strategy**: ask "what job is this hired for?" If the answer is in product features, the team hasn't done the JTBD work.
- **If you're a marketing lead**: JTBD-driven messaging consistently outperforms persona-driven messaging. Bob Moesta has data on this.
- **If you're an engineer or designer**: JTBD analysis often surfaces UX issues you'd otherwise miss. The job framing reveals what the user is *actually* trying to do.

## How thinking has evolved

- **Pre-2022**: Christensen's JTBD canon predates the corpus.
- **2022-2024**: Bob Moesta on Lenny's Podcast — multiple episodes deepening the methodology.
- **2024-2025**: JTBD applied to AI products (corpus and external community).
