# Caution: claims to treat with skepticism on How AI is changing or replacing the PM role

## "The idea of a PM makes no sense in the future"

- **Claim**: "Well, I saw this podcast or I listened to this podcast that Peter Fenn did. And he convinced me that the idea of a PM makes no sense basically in the future. If you think about decomposing the logic, what does PM usually do? They take these inputs from customers, they create this sequential roadmap that's well organized over the next year, blah, blah, blah, blah, blah." Followed by: "The skill is more like being a CEO now, which is what are we building and why?"
- **Said by**: Keith Rabois (Managing Director, Khosla Ventures), [Hard truths about building in the AI era](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xCd9ykretlg), 2026-04-12.
- **Audience pushback (top comments by likes)**:
  - **191 votes**: "fascinating conversation, from the people who will never be affected by any of it. Keith hasn't touched a computer since 2010, manages billions, and is here to explain why your job is dying. No one on this podcast ever invites the senior PM who just got laid off, the developer with a mortgage..." — @TheVimeo
  - **145 votes**: "it is like watching Queen Marie Antoinette explain what farmers should do" — @NotAllHeroesWearCapes-101
  - **57 votes**: "Talks about 'intellectual curiosity' to learn new things but hasn't been using a PC since 2010. Talks about successfully hiring talent by poaching talent within the same company. Talks about building strong teams, but doesn't believe in psych safety. This is not somebody to learn from." — @yoyobroker8107
  - **50 votes**: "Product Managers that aren't asking 'What are we building and why' are product owners or business analysts, not product managers." — @mckirkus (note: this is a corrective, not full agreement; argues Rabois's caricature describes bad PMs, not the role)
  - **47 votes**: "His example for not doing customer feedback is DoorDash, when the DoorDash founder said himself that they shadowed restaurant owners to get the initial idea (seeing a restaurant owner with a book of turned down delivery orders being one piece of important feedback) and does daily customer support to this day." — @FrankDenbowDotCom (factual rebuttal: Rabois's example contradicts DoorDash's own founding story)
  - **34 votes**: "Lenny, genuine question: in 1,600 episodes, why has every guest been someone who profits from disruption — never someone destroyed by it? You've had VCs declare PM dead, engineers replaceable, design automated. But you've never once sat across from the person living that reality." — @TheVimeo (same author, separate comment, calling out selection bias in the podcast)
  - **12 votes**: "Wait didn't you just have the head of growth from anthropic on your podcast saying they might need to hire more PMs but we are supposed to believe this guy???" — @danduhman1608

- **Why this is flagged as a caution** (not just disagreement):
  - **Privilege-blind**: Rabois operates in an environment (Khosla Ventures partner, billion-dollar deals, 25+ years senior) where his prescriptions cost him nothing if wrong. PMs who follow his "PM is dead" framing risk career-altering damage. The 191-vote and 145-vote top comments call out exactly this asymmetry.
  - **Factually wrong on his own example**: Rabois cites DoorDash as evidence customer feedback isn't needed. The DoorDash founder has publicly stated they shadowed restaurant owners and the founder still does daily customer support. The 47-vote rebuttal is verifiable and damaging to the underlying argument.
  - **Internally inconsistent corpus signal**: Same week, Anthropic's head of product (Cat Wu) describes a fully-staffed PM team and an Anthropic head of growth says they need to hire more PMs. Two recent first-hand sources from a leading AI lab directly contradict the "PMs are dead" thesis.
  - **Caricatures the PM role to a strawman**: defining PMs as merely "people who create a sequential roadmap" ignores the judgment-driven craft Singhal, Wu, Doshi, and Cagan all describe in the same corpus. The 50-vote comment captures this.

- **Where (if anywhere) the claim might still apply**:
  - In an organization where PM has degraded into ticket-pusher / status-meeting-runner / Jira-owner roles: Rabois's caricature is accurate to *that pathology* and AI does eat that work. But the rebuttal is to fix the role, not eliminate it. Nikhyl Singhal's "information mover -> dinosaur" framing is the more careful version of the same observation.
  - For a pre-PMF founder shipping their own product: the "you should be the PM, not hire one" advice is sound for that specific stage — but Rabois generalizes far beyond it.

- **Cross-checks**:
  - Same-corpus contrasts: [Cat Wu on PM role at Anthropic, 2026-04-23](../topics/ai-replacing-pms.md#external-current-consensus-web), [Nikhyl Singhal v2, 2026-04-19](../topics/ai-replacing-pms.md), [Lenny's 2026 job market data](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/state-of-the-product-job-market-in-2025) showing PM roles at 3-year high.
  - External authoritative sources: HBR, BCG, and other late-2025/early-2026 industry coverage frames PM role as evolving, not eliminated.

- **Confidence level**: high — the audience pushback is substantial (top comment 191 votes, multiple substantive >30-vote rebuttals including a factual error documentation), AND the corpus has multiple recent authoritative contradictions, AND Rabois's own example is documented to be wrong.

## "No days off" — never miss a workout, never take a day off work, mantra-as-rule

- **Claim**: "I don't believe in taking days off for workout. I don't believe taking days off from work, period." Followed by: "I believe I've missed seven days in seven years of working out. And it still kills me. Half of those I still am annoyed that I missed." Framed as the speaker's mantra-as-rule.
- **Said by**: Keith Rabois, [Hard truths about building in the AI era](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xCd9ykretlg), 2026-04-12, ~01:19:40.
- **Audience pushback**: same episode comment cluster (see top comment list above) flags the speaker's perspective broadly as out of touch. The "no days off" claim was a specific trigger for the 145-vote Marie-Antoinette comment.
- **Why this is flagged as a caution** (not just disagreement):
  - **Normalizes burnout** when generalized. The speaker is a 50-something multi-billion-dollar VC partner with control over his calendar and no childcare/eldercare obligations he discusses. Prescribing his routine as "what successful people do" to a junior PM working 60+ hours under a manager is harmful advice.
  - **Conflates discipline with refusing recovery**. The corpus has multiple counter-examples: Jonny Miller (Nervous System Mastery) on burnout recovery, Hilary Gridley on resilience as a *system* not willpower, the entire 2025-2026 thread on how techs most resilient workers handle burnout. The consensus is the opposite: scheduled recovery improves performance.
  - **Survivorship bias**. Rabois's claim implies his 7-days-a-week-for-7-years routine caused his outcomes. Confounded by extreme inherited and accumulated capital, network access, and a calendar he fully controls.
- **Where (if anywhere) the claim might still apply**:
  - For founders in pre-PMF crunch periods who explicitly choose this tradeoff and own the cost: short-bursts of "no days off" are defensible. Generalizing it to a 7-year mantra is not.
  - For specific physical-training contexts where consistency is high-leverage and load-management is well-managed by a coach: a "every day" practice is defensible. Claiming guilt for the seven missed days in seven years is not the recommended frame in any peer-reviewed sport science.
- **Cross-checks**:
  - Counter-corpus voices: [Jonny Miller, Lenny 2024](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/managing-nerves-anxiety-and-burnout-jonny-miller); Hilary Gridley on team resilience; the "How tech's most resilient workers handle burnout" newsletter (2026).
  - External: extensive sport-science literature on recovery, deload weeks, and parasympathetic activation contradicts the "no days off ever" claim as a generalized health principle.
- **Confidence level**: high — multiple substantial counter-sources in the corpus and external; speaker's framing explicitly generalizes from his single perspective.
