# Good Strategy / Bad Strategy — Richard Rumelt

## Consensus key insights

Rumelt's framework for distinguishing real strategy from "fluff" is the most-cited strategy book in the corpus and (with Helmer's *7 Powers*) one of the two go-to references.

1. **The kernel of strategy: diagnosis + guiding policy + coherent action.**
   - **Diagnosis**: what's actually going on? Causal model of the situation.
   - **Guiding policy**: what we'll do about it. Direction, not detail.
   - **Coherent action**: concrete moves that fit together. The actions reinforce each other.

2. **Bad strategy = "fluff."** Empty slogans, motherhood goals ("we will be best in class"), aspirational statements masquerading as analysis. If the strategy is mostly about *what we want*, not *what's true and what we'll do*, it's bad strategy.

3. **Bad strategy mistakes goals for strategy.** "Increase market share by 30%" is a goal, not a strategy. Strategy explains *how*.

4. **Good strategy is hard because it requires saying no.** Strategy is fundamentally about choosing not to do things. The discomfort of saying no is why bad strategy persists.

5. **Strategy needs to engage the actual situation, not the wished-for situation.** Rumelt's repeated point: most "strategies" don't survive contact with the actual diagnosis.

## Potentially interesting but rarer takes

**[CRITICAL READING REQUIRED]**

- **Rumelt's hyper-competition critique of Mike Porter** (Rumelt's framing on the 2024 episode: he disagrees with Porter on competition stability). Rumelt argues Porter implicitly assumes more competitive stability than reality offers; Porter's defenders argue Rumelt mischaracterizes Porter's positions. Read both before forming your view.
- **"Strategy is just saying no."** A reduction; saying no is necessary but not sufficient. Strategy needs the diagnosis first, then the policy, then the actions.

## Where the consensus may have decayed

- **The book pre-dates AI-era strategic compression.** "Coherent action" still applies but iteration cycles have collapsed; the "set the strategy and execute" cadence Rumelt assumes is faster in 2026 than in 2011.
- **For platform / network products**, Helmer's 7 Powers framework gives more concrete moat language than Rumelt's general framework. Use both.
