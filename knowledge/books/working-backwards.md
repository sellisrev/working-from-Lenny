# Working Backwards — Bill Carr & Colin Bryar

## Consensus key insights

The canonical "how Amazon ships products" account by two former senior Amazon executives.

1. **The Working Backwards process.** Start from the press release for the product you wish you had launched, then work backwards to feasibility. The PR document forces a customer-centric framing; if the press release is boring, the product is wrong.

2. **The 6-page narrative document.** Replace PowerPoint with prose. The first 30 minutes of a meeting is silent reading; then discussion. Carr's emphasis: prose forces clearer thinking than bullets.

3. **Single-threaded leadership.** One person owns the outcome of the initiative full-time. Cf. Lenny's "barrels" framing.

4. **Two-pizza teams.** Small enough that 2 pizzas feed them; durable; cross-functional; outcome-accountable. Cagan's empowered teams overlap heavily.

5. **Bar raisers in hiring.** Independent reviewer ensures hiring quality across the company; a counter to the "hire a friend" pattern. Cf. [hiring-early-team](../topics/hiring-early-team.md).

6. **Customer obsession as the operating principle, not the marketing slogan.** Specific behavioral commitments (e.g., the empty seat at the meeting representing the customer).

## Potentially interesting but rarer takes

**[CRITICAL READING REQUIRED]**

- **"Working Backwards is the only correct way."** Some readers absolutize Amazon's process; Cagan and others argue empowered teams reach similar outcomes via different paths. The PR-FAQ format is a tool, not a principle.
- **"Two-pizza teams is universal."** The framing works for many but not all contexts; very large enterprise products often need broader cross-team coordination than two-pizza teams can manage alone.

## Where the consensus may have decayed

- **The 6-page narrative document** remains useful but lightweight PRDs (cf. [reviewing-prds-and-1-pagers](../topics/reviewing-prds-and-1-pagers.md)) are increasingly favored over heavy 6-pagers.
- **The PR-FAQ format** still shines for new products but is sometimes ceremoniously applied to obvious next-step features where a 1-pager would do. Pick the format to fit the decision weight.
